### 📖 Swedenborg’s Interpretation of John 9
- **Blindness = ignorance of divine truth**  
  For Swedenborg, the man born blind represents those who lack knowledge of spiritual things, often through no fault of their own. This ignorance is not condemned; it is a state of potential waiting for illumination.
- **Healing = enlightenment by divine truth**  
  Jesus’ act of giving sight symbolizes the Lord opening the inner mind to spiritual light. The “mud and washing” are correspondences: earthly means (clay, water) representing divine truth applied to the natural mind, then purified.
- **Conflict with Pharisees = false sight**  
  The elders who interrogate the man represent those who claim to “see” (self‑derived wisdom) but are blind because they reject divine illumination. John 9:39 is central: “those who claim to see become blind.”
- **Exile = separation from false religion**  
  The man’s expulsion from the synagogue symbolizes the rejection of those who embrace divine truth by established religious authorities. Yet exile becomes the gateway to true sight.

### 🌌 Your Interpretation
- **Blindness = karmic question**  
  You frame the disciples’ question as about inherited karma. Barabbas says the blindness is “so God’s power may be revealed.” This aligns with Swedenborg’s idea that ignorance can be the soil for divine illumination.
- **Two healers (Barabbas and Nazareth)**  
  You split the healing: Barabbas applies spit‑mud and sends the man to “the Sent,” while Jesus of Nazareth completes the cure. Swedenborg sees one continuous correspondence, but your version dramatizes the dual Jesus motif — Barabbas as initiator, Nazareth as fulfiller.
- **Trees walking = partial sight**  
  You emphasize the intermediate stage of vision (“people like trees walking”), which Swedenborg would interpret as partial enlightenment — truth seen dimly before full regeneration.
- **Village conflict and exile**  
  You highlight the man’s return, argument, and exile. Swedenborg interprets this as rejection by false religion; you tie it to Ezekiel’s “Son of Man” packing for exile, making the exile itself a prophetic fulfillment.
- **Barabbas as Son of Man**  
  You distinguish Barabbas as “Son of Man” (not Messiah), echoing Ezekiel’s exile imagery. Swedenborg interprets “Son of Man” as the Lord in His role as Divine Truth. Your version reframes it as Barabbas embodying exile and prophetic burden.

### ✨ Resonance and Divergence
- **Resonance**: Both readings see blindness as potential, healing as enlightenment, and exile as paradoxically the path to truth.  
- **Divergence**: Swedenborg keeps Jesus unified as the Divine Truth, while your Two Jesus Thesis splits the roles — Barabbas as initiator/exile‑bearer, Nazareth as fulfiller/healer. Swedenborg’s “Son of Man” is universal divine truth; your “Son of Man” is Barabbas specifically, tied to Ezekiel’s exile imagery.

Both agree that ignorance can be redeemed, partial sight precedes full vision, and exile paradoxically fulfills divine purpose — but they diverge on who embodies “Son of Man” and how the healing unfolds.
