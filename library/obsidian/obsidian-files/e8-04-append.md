### Salome's Education and the Discovery of Sychar (14–20 CE)

As Salome grew, Herod and Herodias hired tutors from far-reaching places to educate her in Greek, Latin, Hebrew, Aramaic, music, dance, rhetoric, and philosophy. Among them was a teacher from the far east, a scholar who had traveled from India to teach the wisdom of the Vedas and the epics of the Mahabharata.

One evening, the tutor sat with Salome in the courtyard, recounting the story of Draupadi, the princess born from fire, who married five brothers and bore five sons. He spoke of her beauty, her strength, and her rage when she was humiliated by Dushasana before the court of kings. 

Salome listened, enthralled.  "Did she ever find justice?" she asked.

The tutor smiled faintly. "In the end, her sons were slaughtered, and she ascended to heaven with her husbands. But some believe her spirit was reborn, not in India, but in a small village in Samaria called Sychar."

Herod, who had been listening from the doorway, stepped forward. "Sychar? The village near Jacob's Well?"

The tutor nodded. "Yes, my lord. It is said that a woman there, called Draupi, bears the same name and the same fire.  She has five sons, just as the Draupadi of old. And her father, Drupa, is a scholar and keeper of ancient wisdom."

Herod's eyes gleamed. "A scholar? A Brahmin?"

"Yes, my lord. A Brahmin priest, though he follows no temple here. He is respected by his people, but forgotten by the world."

Herod turned to Herodias, who had been watching silently. "If this is true," he said, "then we must learn more about this Drupa. If he is a Brahmin, then he possesses spiritual authority. And if he has a grandchild—this Jesusa Bara Bas—then he may be the key to controlling the prophecy."

Herodias nodded slowly. "Send scouts to Sychar. Let them observe, but do not act yet. We must understand the full scope of what we are dealing with."

Herod agreed.  "And in the meantime, I will have my scholars study the Mahabharata. If the roles are reversed—if I am Drona and Drupa is now the Brahmin—then I will take what Drona took. But instead of worldly power, I will take spiritual authority."

Herodias smiled faintly. "Half the kingdom, Herod?"

"Half the spiritual kingdom," Herod corrected. "And when the time is right, I will take the other half as well."
