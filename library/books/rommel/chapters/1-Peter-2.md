1 Therefore you must reject all malice and all deceit, and hypocrisies, and envies, and all slander.
  
[Now evil is that which is intentional and unjustifiable in the eyes of any assembly of saints, and such an assembly knows that each one acts according to his particular knowledge and circumstances.
  
So concerning the notion of deceit, it is why do you what you do and not what you do.]
  
2 Desire ardently the pure spiritual milk which is the word of the Lord, as newborn babies desires breast milk, so that you may grow by it for salvation.
  
3 If you have already tasted that the Lord is good.
  
4 Come closer to him, living stones rejected by men but chosen and precious in the sight of God.
  
5 And you yourselves, being living stones, let each of you edify himself to become a spiritual house, a holy priesthood, in order to offer spiritual sacrifices through Jesus Christ, since it is such offerings that are pleasing to God now.
  
[That is to say, take personal initiatives with the aim of pleasing Jesus Christ who is the Lord sent by God.]
  
6 For it is said in the scripture: Behold I lay in Zion a chief cornerstone, elect, precious.
  
Whoever trusts in his message will not be ashamed.
  
7 So the honor is for you who believe.
  
But for the disbelievers, the stone that the builders have disallowed has been made the head of the corner.
  
8 And a stone of stumbling, and a rock of scandal.
  
They stumble at it for not believing in the word, and that is what they are destined for.
  
9 As for you, you are a chosen kind, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, a people who belong to God, so that you may proclaim the virtues of the one who has called you out of darkness into his marvelous light.
  
10 You who were not a people in the past, and now are the people of God.
  
You who had not obtained mercy, and now have obtained mercy.
  
[Now Peter is writing this letter to a community where there is a majority of non-Jews, and he points out that the Jews and the Jews did not form a single people in the past, more specifically those of the non-Jews who had a certain form of love for justice in themselves but who did not know the exact identity of that God.]
  
11 Beloved, since you are strangers and pilgrims in this world, I beseech you to abstain from fleshy lusts which wage war against the soul.
  
12 You must have a good behavior among the disbelievers and the unbelievers, so that when some of them slander you as if you were wrongdoers [because of your faith in Christ], they may notice your good deeds and glorify God in the day when he visits them [that is to say, the day when they also become the disciples of Christ after having being influenced by your behavior].
  
13 Be submissive to every authority established among men, because of the Lord.
  
[Now being submissive does not mean obeying any kind of order.
  
For a disciple of Christ has the obligation to seek the well-being of others in everything he does, and consequently to disobey any order that is given by any person having a certain authority if that order consists of harming others without publicly acceptable justification, as had been done by the midwives who lived in the kingdom of Egypt (Exodus 1:6-14,15-21).]
  
14 Whether to the king who is the sovereign, or to the governors who are sent by him to punish evildoers and to approve the people who do what is right.
  
15 For it is the will of God that you silence ignorant and foolish men through the good quality of your deeds.
  
16 Conduct yourselves like free people, without using your freedom as a veil that covers wickedness, but acting as servants of God.
  
17 Honor everyone, have brotherly love.
  
Fear God.
  
Honor the king.
  
18 Servants, be submitted to your masters and have the fear of offending them [without publicly acceptable justification], not only toward those who are good and gentle, but also toward those who are of a difficult character.
  
19 For it is a grace to endure afflictions when one suffers unjustly, if the justification for this endurance is the desire to have a good conscience toward God.
  
20 Indeed, what glory is there in enduring mistreatment for having acted in a reprehensible manner.
  
But if you endure suffering when you do what is right, then it is a grace in the sight of God.
  
21 And it is what you have been called to do, since Christ also has suffered for you, leaving you an example so that you should follow in his steps.
  
22 He did not committed any sin, and no deceit was found in his mouth.
  
[Now the purpose of this statement is not to judge the behavior of Jesus Christ in relation to any standard, but rather to state that the behavior of Jesus Christ is the standard that must be used to understand the definition of the notion of sin and of the notion of deception, and this requires a good understanding of the circumstances in which each of his words has been uttered.]
  
23 He did not retaliate when he was insulted, and made no threat when he was mistreated, but relied on the one who judges righteously.
  
24 He himself bore our sins in his body on the wooden cross, so that we may die toward sin [at the different times when each of us gain a good understanding of his message and choose to live in accordance with it], and thus live for righteousness.
  
You have been healed through his wounds.
  
25 For you were like wandering sheep, and now you have returned to the Shepherd and Bishop of your souls.
  
the Shepherd and Bishop of your souls.
