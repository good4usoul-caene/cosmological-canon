1 This saying is trustworthy: If someone aspires to the office of a bishop, then he desires an excellent work.
  
2 For a bishop must be blameless, the husband of one wife, self-restraint, modest, methodical, hospitable, able to teach.
  
3 Not given to wine, avoiding quarrels, but gentle, and peaceable, indifferent to the power of money.
  
4 He must manage his own household honestly, keeping his children submissive and respectful.
  
5 For if someone does not know how to manage his own household, then how will he take care of the church of God.
  
6 He must not be a recent convert, otherwise he may become conceited and fall under the judgment of the devil.
  
7 He must also have a good reputation even from the disbelievers, so that he may not fall into disgrace and into the snare of the devil.
  
8 Likewise, Deacons must be men worthy of respect, not deceitful, not given to much wine, not greedy of gain.
  
9 They must obey with a pure conscience to the true faith that God has now made known to us through Jesus Christ.
  
10 They must first be tested.
  
And then, if they are found blameless, then let them serve.
  
11 Likewise, their wives must be worthy of respect, not slanderers, but self-restraint and faithful in everything.
  
12 A deacons must be husband of one wife, and manage his children and households well.
  
13 For those who fulfill their ministry well obtain an honorable standing, and a great boldness in the faith which is in Christ Jesus.
  
14 I am writing these things to you now, hoping to come to you soon.
  
15 So that you may know, if I delay, how one must behave in the house of God, which is the church of the living God, the pillar and foundation of the truth.
  
16 And without dispute, the mystery of piety is great: Christ has been manifested in the flesh, justified by his spirit, seen by angels, preached to the Gentiles, believed in the world, exalted in glory.
