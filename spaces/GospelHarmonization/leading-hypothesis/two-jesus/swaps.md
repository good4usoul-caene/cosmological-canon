# swaps.md  
Bare minimum heuristic notes for the Two Jesus Hypothesis.  

---

## 📖 Gospel of John – Swap Sequence

 - **Messiah Circumcision**
 -   Simeon in Luke 2 circumcizes baby Jesus of Nazareth, likely "tasting the flesh and drinking the blood" of the Messiah.

- **Son of Man Circumcision (John 13)**  
  - Jesus Barabbas places a morsel in Judas’s mouth — interpreted as the kiss/circumcision.  
  - Four chapters follow: Barabbas’s prayers, heard and recorded by John after captivity.  

- **First Swap (John 18)**  
  - Jesus of Nazareth crosses the Kidron Valley.  
  - He sees Judas circumcising Jesus Barabbas.  
  - Jesus of Nazareth identifies himself.  Men fall back, confused at who is who.
  - Ear of Malchus (foreskin of Barabbas)
  - Simon Peter cuts off the ear of Malchus.  (Simon Peter is later mentioned in Acts in disagreement with Paul, requiring circumcision of gentiles)
  - From here, John follows Jesus of Nazareth.

- **Second Swap (John 19)**  
  - Joseph of Arimathea and Nicodemus collect the corpse of Jesus Barabbas.  
  - Narrative authority shifts — John begins to assume the role of *Maher Shalal Hash Baz*.  
  - **Correction:** The text does *not* swap back to Jesus of Nazareth here.  

- **Threshold (John 20:3–6)**  
  - Mary Magdalene reports the empty tomb.  
  - John runs ahead — “rushes to the spoils” — but does not enter.  
  - Peter arrives second but enters first, asserting apostolic authority.  
  - John enters after and believes, marking the hand‑off: he becomes *Maher Shalal Hash Baz*, seer of aftermath.  

- **John 20–21**  
  - Two near‑blasphemies: fictionalized words attributed to Barabbas.  
  - The Spirit of Jesus acknowledged in another man — possibly Jesus of Nazareth, who did not die.

## 📜 Gospel of Matthew – Swap Sequence

- **Dual Scene (Matthew 26)**  
  - Jesus of Nazareth weeps ordinary tears.  
  - Jesus Barabbas sheds literal blood from circumcision.  
  - Narrative follows Barabbas until crucifixion.  

- **Swap (Matthew 27)**  
  - Nicodemus arrives alone.  
  - He takes the weakened Jesus of Nazareth to a tomb.  
  - From here, Matthew follows Jesus of Nazareth without detours.  
  - He trusts the women who say to find him atop the mountain in Galilee.  

## 🔄 The Shuffle – Composite Scene Across Gospels

- **Interviews**  
  - Jesus of Nazareth with Annas (outside).  
  - Jesus Barabbas with Caiaphas (inside).  

- **Robes and Recognition**  
  - Jesus of Nazareth dressed twice: by Herod and Pilate.  
  - Robes resemble Odysseus’s outfit given to him by Persephone before he sailed to fight battle of Troy.  
  - Pilate presents Barabbas in common robes, Jesus of Nazareth as Odysseus.  

- **Crowd and Beating**  
  - Crowd chooses Barabbas’s release.  (preferring the spiritual "Jesus, Son of the Father" over the royal "Jesus, King of the Jews")
  - Pilate only pretends to release Barabbas, sending both men into a circle of guards.  
  - Barabbas’s face beaten until unrecognizable.  
  - Robe placed on Barabbas; he is taken to crucifixion.
  - Cross carried by Simon the Cyrene, (face beaten, could not see, likely could barely walk)

- **Final Crucifixion**  
  - Hours later, Jesus of Nazareth brought before priests at the Stone Pavement.  
  - Crucified with instructions: his skin not pierced (no nails).
  - Carried his own cross.  Cross carried by Jesus of Nazareth, (had worn crown of thorns and was smacked around a bit, but he was not beaten bloody)
  - He alone is identified as “the Nazarene” in all four Gospels.  
  - Surrounded by the Marys.  
  - One witness claims he was impaled by a spear — insufficient under Jewish law to establish guilt.

## 🪶 Heuristic Summary
“Track the swaps. Name the Nazarene. Follow the robe. Trust the women. Question the spear.”

## 🔍 Interpretive Note
The Maher‑Shalal‑Hash‑Baz threshold marks John’s final narrative alignment: he becomes the recorder of aftermath, the seer of spoils, bearing witness to Barabbas’s burial and the echo of a spirit beyond death.
