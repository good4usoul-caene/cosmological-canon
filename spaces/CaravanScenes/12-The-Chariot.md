## Chapter 12: The Chariot (~26 CE)

### The Upa Pandavas Visit Capernaum (John 7:1-9)

**1** After this, Jesus Barabbas stayed in Galilee.     He would not walk in Judea because the Jewish leaders there were looking for a way to kill him.  

**2** But when the Jewish Festival of Tabernacles was near,  

**3** the Upa Pandavas — Yudhishthira, Bhima, Arjuna, Nakula, and Sahadeva — traveled from their fishing village to Capernaum to see their youngest sibling.  

They found Jesus Barabbas in the synagogue, kneeling beside the clay model of Jerusalem.   Figurines stood arrayed around the temple courtyard — some sniffing the branch, some making murals of bugs and nature, some weeping for Tammuz, some facing east with their backs to the temple, worshiping the sun.  

Yudhishthira, the eldest, cleared his throat.   "Bara, we need to talk."

Barabbas looked up, mud on his hands.  "Brothers.    What brings you here?"

Bhima, the strong one, laughed.   "We came to drag you out of this studio.    You've been hiding here for weeks."

Arjuna, the precise one, gestured at the clay model.  "This is beautiful work.   But if you want to be a public figure, you can't just sit here preaching to dolls."

**4** Nakula said, "No one who wants to become a public figure acts in secret.  Since you are doing these things, show yourself to the world."

Sahadeva, the youngest of the five, added quietly, "The Festival of Tabernacles is coming.    All of Judea will be gathered in Jerusalem.  If you have a teaching to give, give it there."

Yudhishthira leaned forward.  "Besides, Bara — you've performed *one* miracle.  The man at Bethesda.   He's healed, yes.   But Jesus of Nazareth has healed dozens.  Hundreds, some say.   If you stay silent, how will anyone know you?"

Arjuna nodded.  "Won't you at least consider rejoining the Caravan of the Fifth Wind?  They all miss you terribly."

**5** (For even his own brothers did not believe in him — at least, not in the way the crowds did.    They saw him as their youngest sibling, brilliant but eccentric, prone to theatrical stunts.)

**6** Barabbas stood, brushing clay dust from his hands.  "The right time for me has not yet come; for you any time will do.    **7** The world cannot hate you, but it hates me because I testify that its works are evil. 

"And you think this will help." Arjuna said (the precise one), "testifying of all the sins to a clay model of Jerusalem.  No one will ever hear that."

"Indeed, I suppose not." said Barabbas, "Yet it's the role of the Son of Man."

"Making a list of every sin that anyone can die from?  And preaching to a clay model of Jerusalem?  What sort of God do you worship that thinks that will be of any use?"

Jesus Barabbas sighs.  "I am to be held accountable for any such sin that I do not list.  It seems a futile task."

Yudhisithra, the eldest, said, "Consider a list of the most deadly sins.  Then come to the Festival of Tabernacles to warn of them."
 
Barabbas shakes his head.  **8** You go to the festival.    I am not going up to this festival, because my time has not yet fully come."

Bhima frowned.  "Then you'll just stay here?    Alone with your model?"

Barabbas smiled.  "Yes, but I'll send something with you."

---

### The Envelope (John 7:10-13)

Barabbas went to his writing desk and pulled out parchment, sealing wax, and a stylus.   He folded the parchment carefully, sealed it with wax, and wrote on the outside: 

**From:** *Jesus, Son of Man*  
*Studio in Capernaum Temple, Galilee*

**To:** *Festival of Tabernacles, Jerusalem*  
*To whom it may concern*

He handed the sealed envelope to Yudhishthira.   "Deliver this to the temple courtyard during the festival.   But do not open it until the last and greatest day."

Nakula hefted the envelope.  "What's inside?"

"A teaching," Barabbas said.  "And an invitation.  But to open it is to kill it.  The envelope lives as long as it remains sealed.  Once opened, the message is revealed — and the mystery dies."

Arjuna frowned.  "You're speaking in riddles again."

"Then let the riddle live," Barabbas said.  "Carry it to Jerusalem.   Let people argue about it.   Let them wonder what it contains.   The envelope itself is the teaching — until you open it."

**9** After he had said this, he stayed in Galilee.

**10** However, after his brothers had left for the festival, the envelope went also — not publicly, but as if in secret.

---

### At the Festival — Before Opening (John 7:11-36)

**11** Now at the festival the Jewish leaders were watching for Jesus Barabbas and asking, "Where is he?"

Odys greeted them and said, "You've heard of some of our plays in Egypt, and want to arrest our 'Son of Man' character?  Is that it?  Don't worry, we won't be performing these plays at the festival."

**12** Among the crowds there was widespread whispering about him.  Some said, "He is a good man."

Others replied, "No, he deceives the people."  Some of them were trying to recover the treasuries of Caligula and Philip.  **13** But no one would say anything publicly, about him for fear of the leaders.

---

**14** Not until halfway through the festival did the Upa Pandavas arrive in Jerusalem.  They went up to the temple courts and held up the sealed envelope. 

Yudhishthira announced:   "This is from Jesus Barabbas, Son of Man.  He sends his teaching from Capernaum."

**15** John asked, "How did this man get such learning without having been taught?"

Yudhisitthra said, "That is so, John, I have learning here in this envelope.  But I have not been taught.  In fact, I have not even yet seen the learning.  This is the miracle of the envelope."

**16** Bhima, reading from the outside of the envelope, said:   

"From Jesus Barabbas, Son of Man.   We know where this letter comes from.   We know the address.   The teaching is not from paper.   It comes from the one who sent it."

**17** A voice from the crowd:   "Anyone who chooses to do his own will should ask whether his choice comes from within, from God, or from others."

**18** Another voice:  "Whoever speaks on their own does so to gain personal glory, but he who seeks the glory of the one who sent him is a man of truth; there is nothing false about him."

Arjuna said, "The piece of paper in this evelope seeks the glory of the one who sent it.  So then by your reasoning, there can be nothing false about it."

**19** Then the man from Bethesda with the mat stepped up.  "Has not Moses given us the law?  Yet not one of you keeps the law.    This man, Jesus, healed on the Sabbath. Why are you trying to kill him?"

**20** "You are demon-possessed," someone in the crowd answered.  "Who is trying to kill him?"

"I wish people would stop accusing me," he said.  "You can see I walk with with steady legs.  I am no longer lame. I met this man, He healed me at the pool of Bethesda.  Told me to take up my mat and walk.   I did.  Then he told me to stop sinning lest anything worse would come to me.  It's been months now, and I haven't limped since."

A Pharisee scoffed.  "One miracle. Jesus of Nazareth has performed dozens.   Why should we listen to this Barabbas?"

The healed man said, "Because he tells you to stop sinning, or else."

**21** Bhima (the Strong one) raised his voice:    "Jesus Barabbas performed one sign on the Sabbath, and you are all amazed.   

**22** Yet, because Moses gave you circumcision (though actually it did not come from Moses, but from the patriarchs), you circumcise a boy on the Sabbath.  

**23** Now if a boy can be circumcised on the Sabbath so that the law of Moses may not be broken, why are you angry with him for healing a man's whole body on the Sabbath?  

The temple worker said, "I wasn't really so much angry, but concerned because he literally said, unlike your father, my father works on the Sabbath!  According to our law any prophet who speaks for a different God than Jahweh must be stoned to death." 

Bhima said, **24** Stop judging by mere appearances, but instead judge correctly.

The temple worker said, "Unfortunately, I hardly have anything other than appearances to work with.  I can only wait to see see what this letter says."

---

**25** At this point some of the people of Jerusalem began to ask, "Isn't this the one they are trying to kill?    

**26** Here he is, speaking publicly (through this envelope), and they are not saying a word to him.   Have the authorities really concluded that the Son of Man is the Messiah?   

**27** But we know where this man is from — Capernaum.   When the Messiah comes, no one will know where he is from."

**28** Sahadeva held up the envelope and cried out:    

"Yes, you know where he is from — **the address is written on the envelope!**   The envelope is not here on his own authority, but he who sent it is true.    You do not know him,  

**29** but we know him because we are from him and he sent us — **with this envelope**."

**30** At this they tried to seize the envelope, but no one laid a hand on it, because its hour had not yet come.

**31** Still, many in the crowd believed in him.  They said, "When the Messiah comes, will he perform more signs than this Son of Man?"

**32** The Pharisees heard the crowd whispering such things about him.  Then the chief priests and the Pharisees sent temple guards to arrest him — or at least seize the envelope. 

---

**33** Nakula held up the envelope again and announced:  

"Jesus Barabbas says:  'I am with you for only a short time, and then I am going to the one who sent me.   You will look for me, but you will not find me; and where I am, you cannot come. '"

**34** The Jews said to one another, "Where does this man intend to go that we cannot find him?   Will he go where our people live scattered among the Greeks, and teach the Greeks?  

**35** What did he mean when he said, 'You will look for me, but you will not find me,' and 'Where I am, you cannot come'?"

**36** The crowd argued about the envelope for three more days.   Some wanted it opened immediately.    Others insisted it should remain sealed.  

"To open it is to kill it," one man said.  "The envelope lives as long as the mystery remains."

"But what good is a message no one can read?" another replied.

"The message is the envelope itself," the healed man from Bethesda said.  "It tells us:   Jesus Barabbas sends word from Capernaum.    That is the teaching.   The rest is commentary."

---

### The Opening — Rivers of Living Water (John 7:37-44)

**37** On the last and greatest day of the festival, Yudhishthira stood up and shouted:  

"It is time."

He broke the wax seal.   

The crowd fell silent.

He unfolded the parchment.

Inside was a single blank page.

At the top, in Barabbas's handwriting, were these words:

> **"Let anyone who is thirsty come and drink.**  
> **Whoever believes in me, as Scripture has said, rivers of living water will flow from within them."** *(John 7:37-38)*  
>   
> *If you can read this, you are literate.*  
> *If you are literate, you carry responsibility.*  
> *Write what you believe.    Write what you hope.*  
> *Write what you know to be true, or what you suspect might be.*  
>   
> *Let this page be a table from heaven — a place where many voices gather.*  
> *Not one prophet, but many.*  
> *Not one teacher, but all who dare to teach.*  
>   
> *— Jesus Barabbas*

Arjuna read the instructions aloud.   A crowd pressed forward.  

A scribe stepped forward.  "Is this...   a blank scroll?"

Nakula nodded.  "Yes.   He invites you to write on it."

**38** "This is what he meant," the healed man from Bethesda said.  "The rivers of living water — they flow from *us*.    We are the ones who write.    We are the ones who teach."

**39** By this Barabbas meant the Spirit, which those who believed in him were later to receive.   Up to that time the Spirit had not been given, since Jesus Barabbas had not yet been glorified.

**40** On hearing these words, some of the people said, "Surely this man is the Prophet."

**41** Others said, "He is the Messiah."

Still others asked, "How can the Messiah come from Galilee?    

**42** Does not Scripture say that the Messiah will come from David's descendants and from Bethlehem, the town where David lived?"

**43** Thus the people were divided because of Jesus Barabbas.  

**44** Some wanted to seize the scroll, but no one laid a hand on it. 

---

### The Guards Return (John 7:45-52)

**45** Finally the temple guards went back to the chief priests and the Pharisees, who asked them, "Why didn't you bring him in?"

**46** "No one ever spoke the way this man does," the guards replied.  "We couldn't arrest an envelope.   And once we opened it, all we found was a blank page and an invitation."

**47** "You mean he has deceived you also?" the Pharisees retorted.  

**48** "Have any of the rulers or of the Pharisees believed in him?  

**49** No!    But this mob that knows nothing of the law — there is a curse on them."

**50** Nicodemus, who had gone to Jesus earlier and who was one of their own number, asked,  

**51** "Does our law condemn a man without first hearing him to find out what he has been doing?"

**52** They replied, "Are you from Galilee, too?    Look into it, and you will find that a prophet does not come out of Galilee."

Nicodemus said quietly, "Yet we have heard from him.   And the page is blank."

The Pharisees turned away. 

---

### Epilogue:   The Blank Page Fills

By the end of the festival, the blank page was covered in handwriting — dozens of voices, dozens of teachings.  

Some wrote blessings.  Some wrote curses.  Some wrote questions.  

 - "*Is Blue the most royal color?*
 - "*Why has God made me this way?*"
 - "*I want to ride a horse someday.*"
 - "*If God made everything, who made God?*"

Others had copied from Jesus of Nazareth
 - *"Love God with all your heart, all your mind, and all your strength, and love your neighbor as yourself."* (John 6:63)
 - *"See with your eyes, hear with your ears, understand with your hearts, and turn, and be healed."*
 - "*It is not what goes into the mouth that defiles you, but what comes out."*
 - "*Our Father, hallowed be thy name, thy kingdom come on earth as it is in heaven.*" 

The healed man from Bethesda wrote:  
 - *"I was lame.    He asked me:   'Do you want to be healed? '   I said I was lonely.   He healed me, but I am still alone."*

A Pharisee wrote:  
> *"Thou shalt not take the Lord thy God's name in vain."*

A woman wrote:  
> *"My son was healed by Jesus of Nazareth.   But this man's word also carries weight.   Are there two messiahs?"*

The Upa Pandavas rolled up the scroll and returned to Capernaum. 

When they handed it to Barabbas, he unrolled it, read every word, and smiled.

"The Chariot has rolled," he said.   "Now the wheels are in motion."
