## Chapter 15: The Hermit (~27 CE)

### Scene 1: The Eviction (Capernaum Studio) — Past Tense

**Setting:**  
Jesus Barabbas's studio in Capernaum.     The clay model of Jerusalem sits in the center of the room, with figurines scattered around it.   The door is locked from the inside.  

A knock on the door.    No answer.  

The Pharisees, frustrated, climbed through the window.  

Barabbas looked up from the clay model.   "You didn't use the gate."

One of the Pharisees picked up a figurine.     "What is this?"

Barabbas smiled.   "A sheep."

The Pharisee frowned.   "We're here to ask you to leave Capernaum."

"Why?" Barabbas asked.  

"Your teaching has a corrupting influence," the Pharisee said.   "You speak in riddles that lead people astray."

Barabbas debated.   The exchange took several minutes.  John sat in the corner with his scroll and stylus, writing furiously in shorthand, trying to capture every word.

When the debate ended, they packed up their bags, said their farewells, and left town, trying to decide where to go.

### Scene 2: The Bench (Just Outside Capernaum) — Present Tense

**Setting:**  
A bench just outside Capernaum.    Jesus Barabbas, the ex-blind man, Salome, John, Andrew, and the Upa Pandavas sit together.   The sun is low in the sky, casting long shadows. 

Salome says, "John, pull out that scroll again.   We were so rushed I barely had time to think about the riddle."

John unrolls the scroll and begins reading from his shorthand notes.

"Very truly I tell you Pharisees, anyone who does not enter the sheep pen by the gate, but climbs in by some other way, is a thief and a robber..."

He continued to read and finishes, "Jesus used this figure of speech, but they did not understand."

Salome asked "What figure of speech was that?"

John grins, "He was being literal.  Well.  Essentially.  He was describing what a gate does."

Salome says, "So what did he say next?"

John reads, "Truly, truly, I tell you I am the gate for the sheep."

Salome said, "Truly?" She turns to Jesus Barabbas.  "You're a gate for the sheep?"

Barabbas smiled, "And what figure of speech is that?"

Salome said, "That's one of your 'What-Am-I Riddles'"

Barabbas said, "Right."

John continued reading.

Salome said, "So, the properties of a gate; well, a primitive one, anyway.  It lays down its life..."

"Figuratively speaking..." said Arjuna.  

Salome said, "It lays down its life to let the sheep in and out, and lifts it up again to keep the sheep secure."

Barabbas nodded.  "And the gate does this without thinking, without choosing, without running from wolves.    It simply does what it was made to do."

Salome says, "So the gate is more faithful than the shepherd?"

Barabbas smiled.   "In a way, yes."

Salome says, "And how are you and your father one?"

Barabbas said, "Aristotle's final cause."

Yuddhishithra said, "Yes, the final cause is the purpose of the thing.  A gatemaker's purpose in making a gate is the same as the gate's purpose in existing."

Salome says, "Father and son are one, then."

"Now what did you mean 'The sheep listen to your voice?'"

Barabbas said, "They hear the gate creak open, morning or night, and they know it's time to run through."

John said, "You're giving it away."  

Barabbas said, "Well, I stated the answer to the riddle from the very beginning.  I told them, and they didn't believe me."

Salome laughed, "If you testified about yourself, your testimony would never be true."

Shaitana asked, "What did you mean when you said ALL that come before you are thieves and robbers?"

Barabbas said, "Even the owners of the pen come to steal the sheep's wool, and kill them for mutton.  But the gate does none of this."

"And what about the hired hand?"

Barabbas said, "Well, the gate, being inanimate, never runs from wolves.  But a human shepherd will run if a bad enough threat comes along."

John said, "You say that the gate wants more sheep in the pasture?  When did the gate become a monopolist?"

Barabbas said, "Now I was projecting the gate-maker's purpose onto the gate."

Arjuna said, "Because you had earlier claimed they were one."

Barabbas said, "You might question my logic on that.  But doesn't it hold?  A gate should desire to hold as many sheep as the fence would allow."

Arjuna shrugged.

John reads on, finishing with, "These are not the sayings of a man possessed by a demon.  Can a demon open the eyes of the blind?"

Salome laughs.   "Even the defenders don't understand the riddle.   They're defending him because of the blind man, not because they understood."

Barabbas sighed.  "I told them I was a gate.    They heard 'shepherd.'   They heard 'messiah.'  They heard 'healer'.   Even if I tell them the answer, they hear everything except the answer."


They paused.  **22**   Salome shivered.  "It's getting cold."

Barabbas said, "Well, the Festival of Dedication is coming up...  Shall we take a brisk walk to Jerusalem?"

### Scene 3: Festival of Dedication (Jerusalem) — 

**23** And soon, Jesus and his friends were in the temple courts walking in Solomon's Colonnade.  

The Festival of Dedication was in full swing.   Lights flickered in the temple courts, prayers were chanted, and crowds gathered to celebrate the rededication of the temple after the Maccabean Revolt. 

Barabbas, Salome, John, and the Upa Pandavas walked through Solomon's Colonnade, trying to stay warm in the winter chill.

**24** The Jews who were there — some of them from Capernaum — gathered around him, saying, "How long will you keep us in suspense?    If you are the Messiah, tell us plainly."

Barabbas repeated the riddle.  

"I already told you, I am the gate for the sheep, and you did not believe me."

The Jews stared at him.

"You're a gate?"

"Yes," Barabbas said.   "I am the gate for the sheep."

**25** Jesus answered, "I did tell you, but you do not believe.   The works I do in my Father's name testify about me, **26** but you do not believe because you are not my sheep.   **27** My sheep listen to my voice; I know them, and they follow me."

Again, John pulled out his pen and scroll and began furiously scribbling. 

After they were chased out of the festival, Barabbas explained, "The sheep of the riddle know the sound that the gate of the riddle makes."

He pointed to the scroll in John's hand. **28** "The riddle lasts eternally, and so do the sheep and the gate of the riddle."

Barabbas said, "The sheep of the riddle are given to the gate of the riddle by the gate-maker of the riddle.     The gate-maker and the gate are of one purpose."

**31** John said, "They were fixing to pick up stones to throw at you."

Barabbas said, "But for which good work of a gate did they stone me?  The gate-maker has given the sheep a safe place at night, and pasture during the day.   The gate-maker has given the shepherd a way to relax at night.     The gate-maker has given the monopolist a place to store his wealth."

John said, "But they did not even acknowledge that you were claiming to be a gate.   They accused you of claiming to be God."

Barabbas laughed.   "I claimed to be a gate for the sheep.  They accused me of claiming to be God.  But why not?  Scripture says the gods are blind.    Is not a gate blind?  A gate has no heart or mind, yet it serves the purpose of the one who made it.  And the one who made it serves God — whether directly or indirectly, whether willingly or unwillingly."

John reads...  **36** what about the one whom the Father set apart as his very own and sent into the world?    Why then do you accuse me of blasphemy because I said, 'I am God's Son'?"  What are you saying here, Barabbas?

Barabbas said, "These scriptures say that the gods are blind.   I am giving inanimate objects the right to be called 'gods'.     I am personifying inanimate objects, breathing life into them."

He paused.  

"I never said 'I am God's Son'.  That's a false accusation."

You literally said, "'I am God's Son'" said John.

"You're taking me out of context.  They accused me of blasphemy because I said 'I am God's Son'"

You literally said, "I said 'I am God's Son'"

Jesus sighed, "You're taking it all out of context.  Now you're accusing me of blasphemy."


John laughed.  "I'm not accusing you of anything.  I'm just pointing out that your words can be twisted."

Barabbas smiled.  "Then twist them back.  Tell them I'm a gate."

John said, "It was Aaron's task to confess all the sins of Israel over the head of the scapegoat."

Jesus says, "Ah, so now am I Azazel, as well?"

John laughed, "Thomas couldn't pronounce what you are."

John continued reading, 

"**37** Do not believe me unless I do the works of my Father.   **38** But if I do them, even though you do not believe me, believe the works, that you may know and understand that the Father is in me, and I in the Father."

Barabbas said, "Do not believe I am a gate unless I do the work desired by a gate-maker.   But if I do the works desired by a gate-maker, believe the works.     A gate-maker may use me."

John said, "You're a gate, a sphinx, a warner, and a scapegoat.  The way, and the truth, and the light.  They'll try to get you, but you'll always escape their grasp."

### Scene 4: Journey to John's Hometown (Across the Jordan) — Present Tense

**Setting:**  
They continued north, walking along the Jordan River, approaching the place where John the Baptist baptized years ago.   The sun was setting, casting a golden glow over the water. 

They arrived at the place where John the Baptist baptized, at Bethany, on the other side of the Jordan. (about 15 miles northeast of Jerusalem)   People recognized John and come to greet him.

People asked him about who he was traveling with.  He pointed to Barabbas, and said  "This is Jesus Barabbas.  He is from the earth belongs to the earth, and speaks as one from the earth."

Barabbas says, "Unlike Jesus of Nazareth.  The one who is from heaven is above all."

They asked John, "Didn't you used to baptize?"

John smiled, "I used to baptize in water, but Barabbas will baptize you with the Father and Son."

"Who are the Father and Son?" they asked.

Jesus Barabbas knelt down to the soil.  "Speaking as one from earth?  Mother is soil, Father is seed, S...  Son is plant...  John, we still need your water."

John said, "And we still need the Holy Spirit, too.  Riddles and Water can only take you so far."

"I may not know the Holy Spirit," said Jesus, "but by the Spirit of Truth, the seeds need wind, and the green plants need sunlight as well."

Salome adds, "And holiness can't go far without truth."
