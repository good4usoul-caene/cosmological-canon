**[Alexandria marketplace. The Caravan of the Fifth Wind winds through stalls of scrolls, spices, and glassware. Jesusa Bara Bas pauses at a booth where an old man demonstrates a curious lens.]**

**Jesusa (awed):**  
“This is a miracle — the words appear as if written by a hidden hand.”

**Lens-seller (chuckling):**  
“No miracle, child. The same lens can magnify when held close to the page, or make an image more distant when held close to the eye.”

**Jesusa (tilting her head):**  
“Sir, with respect, consistency and flexibility only makes salvation more miraculous. But what use is it to make an image further distant?”

**Lens-seller (smiling, tapping his temple):**  
“Because an old man’s eyes like mine are too weak to wrestle with tiny letters on a looming page.  
But with large letters on a chalk board, bigger and further away, my weak eyes can rest.”

**Jesusa (nodding, delighted):**  
“Ah, *bigger* and further away. I see.”

**[The old man laughs, pinches his fingers together in front of her face, squinting through them.]**  
“What is big, what is little? I squish your head.”

**[Jesusa bursts into laughter, mimicking the gesture.]**  
“Hey vendor, don’t panic — I’m only crushing your head.”

**Vendor: (chuckling)**
“Please, it's Didymus.  And I'm not worried.  You're crushing the angular measure, not the lateral measure.”

“I want two for my grandfather, and one for my lighting guy.”

**Didymus (grinning, counting on his fingers):**  
“Well, I like to invest my money into shipping, or at least put it in my mattress, or my wallet.  
It will be 200 talents each, plus tax — that is 666 talents.”

**Jesusa (gasp):**  
“Oh, wow. Okay. I’ll rename your business *Sparkling Sea Piracy and a Flat-Head*.  
I doubt if the Caravan can raise that much money.”

**Didymus (soberly, folding his hands):**  
“Understand, dear lady, these are the only three lenses I have, and I don’t have the means to reproduce them.  
To me each one is priceless. But even pricelessness has a price.  
With 600 talents, I could commission a ship to return to where I got them, and buy a hundred more.”

**[Jesusa tilts her head, recalling Solomon’s tribute of 666 talents, the Queen of Sheba’s wealth, and the burden of kings.]**  
“But failing that, how about this?  
If the Caravan offers you a permanent place, and you give us just one talent in return —  
your talent as a lighting guy —  
then together with some of the finest minds in Egypt and Rome, you can continue your research in the method of reproduction for the lenses.”

**Didymus (softening, with a smile):**  
“It’s worth considering. I’ll meet with the head of your caravan to see if our goals are compatible.”

**Jesusa:**
It is usually Balzar who decides where we go, but it's at the request of Joseph that we've come down here to Alexandria.  You might also talk to Odys, the Vintner, and Durvasini, the healer.  

**Didymus:**
"Excellent, child.  I will follow you."

And so Bara gained her first disciple.

# 🎭 Honor and Dwelling to Job, the Son of Sorrow
*A play performed by the Caravan of the Fifth Wind in Egypt*

---

## Act I – The Wager

**[Stage: A rustic throne for God, a shadowy corner for the Devil, and Job in bright robes at center. A cow costume lingers at the side, ready for comic effect.]**

**Job (beaming, arms wide):**  
“Oh my, my life is wonderful, my life is so great. Thank good God!”

**God (leaning forward, smug):**  
“Hey Devil, have you considered my servant Job? What a great guy!”

**Devil (snickering):**  
“Ha! But what would he do if you took all your favors away? I bet he would curse you to your face.”

**God (clapping hands):**  
“Wonderful. Take everything he has, but do not touch the man.”

**[The Devil prances across the stage, tapping each character — including the cow — who collapses dramatically.]**

**Job (clutching his chest, still smiling):**  
“That hurts, God, but I will worship you so long as I live.”

**Devil (mock‑innocent):**  
“See? Yes, now. But take away his health.”

**God (shrugging):**  
“Fine. You may torture him eternally. And you may take full control of his wealth. In fact, I won’t put a time limit on this at all. I expect him never to curse me to my face. Never let him die.”

**Devil (rubbing hands together, gleeful):**  
“Excellent! Eternal torture it is, then.”

**[Job staggers, covered in grey cloth, scratching sores, yet still raising his hands in worship. The cow costume twitches back to life, mooing mournfully. The audience laughs at the grotesque repetition.]**

---

## Act II – The Whirlwind and the Toenails

**[Stage: Job sits in ashes. A large paper‑maché whirlwind prop is rolled in by two clowns, spinning it with sticks. God lounges at the side of the stage, idly clipping toenails, counting sheep on an abacus.]**

**God (muttering to himself):**  
“One sheep, two sheep… oh, my nails are uneven.”

**[The Devil leaps into the whirlwind, arms flailing, voice booming.]**

**Devil (from inside the whirlwind):**  
“Would you discredit my justice? Would you condemn me to justify yourself? How dare you accuse me, Job!”

**Job (bewildered, scratching sores):**  
“But I only asked why I suffer. I never cursed you.”

**[The whirlwind spins faster, the Devil’s voice echoing.]**

**Devil (accuser’s tone):**  
“Silence! Your questions are blasphemy. Your testimony is invalid. Your suffering is proof of your guilt!”

**[Meanwhile, God yawns, drops a toenail clipping, and waves vaguely.]**

**God (absent‑minded):**  
“Yes, yes, whatever the whirlwind says. Now, where was I? Three sheep, four sheep…”

**[The comforters enter one by one, each echoing their blasphemous names, adding to the cacophony. Their voices overlap, drowning Job’s protests.]**

- **Eliphaz (“God serves at the right hand of Gold”):** “Your pain is hidden sin. Praise the gold!”  
- **Bildad (“Son of Contention and Prostration”):** “Bow lower, Job, bow until you vanish!”  
- **Zophar (“Chirping Pleasantries”):** “Chirp praises, chirp praises, chirp praises!”  
- **Job’s Wife (“Hated Woman”):** “Curse God and die already!”  
- **Elihu (“My God Blesses Contempt”):** “Your words are folly. Accept divine contempt!”

**[The whirlwind roars, the Devil laughs, and Job collapses under the noise. God keeps counting sheep, oblivious.]**

---

## Act III – The Contract

**[Stage: Job sits in ashes, comforters yammering, Devil inside the whirlwind roaring accusations. God lounges at the side, clipping toenails, counting sheep. Suddenly He pauses, looking troubled.]**

**God (muttering, half‑concerned):**  
“Oh dear… I should do something. My servant Job is suffering terribly.”

**[He picks up a scroll — the “terms of the wager” — and squints at the fine print.]**

**God (reading aloud, puzzled):**  
“Clause 7: ‘Do not intervene.’ Clause 8: ‘No time limit.’ Clause 9: ‘Job must never die.’”

**[He sighs, sets the scroll down, and shrugs.]**

**God (resigned, returning to his toenails):**  
“Well then… my hands are tied. Carry on, whirlwind.”

**[The Devil cackles inside the whirlwind, comforters chant their blasphemous names, and Job collapses under the noise. God resumes counting sheep.]**

---

## Act IV – The Reward

**[Stage: Job sits in ashes, comforters’ voices fading. The whirlwind slows, and the Devil steps out, grinning.]**

**Job (weak but resolute):**  
“But now I see you. Therefore I despise myself and repent in dust and ashes.”

**God (startled, dropping his toenail clippers):**  
“Well… I didn’t think of that. If he had cursed me to my face, I could have settled the bet. But since he cursed the devil…”

**[The Devil bows theatrically, then embraces Job.]**

**Devil (gleeful):**  
“Now you understand God! I will richly reward you, Job. Your wealth, your health, your fortune — all mine to give!”

**[God fumbles with the scroll of the wager, muttering.]**

**God (reading aloud, frustrated):**  
“Clause 4… ‘The Devil has full control over Job’s fortune.’ Shoot. Can he do that? Yes… apparently he can.”

**[The Devil crowns Job with gold chains, pours coins from a sack, and parades him around the stage. The comforters clap awkwardly, unsure whether to praise God or the Devil.]**

**Narrator (deadpan):**  
“And so Job was rewarded — not by God, but by the Accuser. The contract was fulfilled, the wager unresolved, and the whirlwind rolled offstage, still spinning in circles.”
