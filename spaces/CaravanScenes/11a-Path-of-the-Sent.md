## Part 1: The Map of the Sent (~26 CE)

### Jesus of Nazareth Withdraws from Cana (Matthew 14:13, Mark 6:30-32, Luke 9:10)

Jesus of Nazareth knew that the accusations were false, yet the trick must not be revealed.   John was alive and well in Bethesda.   Herod was unknowingly innocent of John's death.  Yet sad and angry men were pouring into Tiberias to protest.   

He gathered his disciples Matthew, James, Peter and Andrew, and others, telling them they would go to Tiberias to calm the crowd, and appeal to their better angels.  

As he traveled, he heard whispers of riot, but he gathered up women and children and those men who had stayed behind with no stomach for violence.  Word spread quickly, and the crowds followed him, mourning the loss of their prophet, John. 

As he traveled, Jesus mentioned to some among the crowd, "My disciples and I will be traveling on to Bethsaida by boat.   It is a beautiful place, and outside Herod's Jurisdiction.  You might like to encourage your friends, husbands and fathers to come that way."

The feelings were intense when they arrived in Tiberias, but the women and children intermingled with the crowd of angry men.  Some, at first, viewed a retreat from the Praetorian bullies as cowardly, but soon the idea of withdrawing to Bethsaida to follow Jesus of Nazareth became the consensus.

So Jesus boarded a boat privately to cross to the eastern shore, seeking solitude near Bethsaida.  But the crowds heard where he was going and followed him on foot from the towns around the lake.

---

### Jesus of Nazareth Feeds Five Thousand (Matthew 14:14-21, Mark 6:33-44, Luke 9:11-17)

When Jesus landed and saw the large crowd, he had compassion on them and healed their sick.  

As evening approached, the disciples came to him and said, "This is a remote place, and it's already getting late. Send the crowds away, so they can go to the villages and buy themselves some food."

Jesus replied, "They do not need to go away. You give them something to eat."

"We have here only five loaves of bread and two fish," they answered.

"Bring them here to me," he said.

And he directed the people to sit down on the grass. Taking the five loaves and the two fish and looking up to heaven, he gave thanks and broke the loaves. Then he gave them to the disciples, and the disciples gave them to the people. 

They all ate and were satisfied, and the disciples picked up twelve basketfuls of broken pieces that were left over.   The number of those who ate was about five thousand men, besides women and children.

---

### Belzar Summons Jesus of Nazareth

During the meal, all anger of John's life ended prematurely had turned toward mourning, and the mourning had turned toward melancholy, and the melancholy was shifting into celebration:  Celebration of a brief life lived well.   As well as a mortal man could do.

After the meal, Jesus was walking among the celebrating crowd when he had the spark of long-past recognition.  Belzar, Odys, Durvasini all mingling among the crowd.   He saw the faded tents of their Caravan in the distance. 

"Odys!   Durvasini!  Can that be you?"

Durvasini said, "You can tell for twenty years of wrinkles?   It is good to see you again, Jesus."  

"Your appearance may be different, but I could feel your hearts from a hundred feet away.  You must all tell me of Egypt," said Jesus.  

"We will.   Oh.   This is Didymus.   He's been traveling with us since Alexandria."

"Didymus...   That means..."

"Twin," Didymus said, "You could call me Gemini or Thomas, I suppose.   I've always rather had a liking for Thomas.  And you are Jesus, Son of Mary?"

Jesus' eyes widened, "Is she back, too?"

"Yes, She is, she is!   She's in our tent with the others!" all four exclaimed.  Didymus continued, "And Matthew and I wish to speak with you about signs for the Kingdom of Heaven."

Jesus smiled, "Some sign beyond this moment of peace and happiness and contentment?"

"Yes," said Thomas.  "Can your Lord send down to us a table spread with food from the heaven?"

Jesus laughed.   "If those who lead you say to you, 'See, the kingdom is in the sky,' then the birds of the sky will precede you.   If they say to you, 'It is in the sea,' then the fish will precede you.  Rather, the kingdom is inside of you, and it is outside of you."

Didymus said, "That is true wisdom.   I will write that down immediately."

"Come to the tent with us and at least hear the signs we have in mind," said Belzar.  "John is there, and your old friend Jesusa Bara Bas...   Now he goes by Jesus Barabbas.   Matthew saw us trying to sneak him in, and recognized John immediately."

Jesus of Nazareth gritted his teeth.  "If the wrong person should enter the tent with John, he will be immediately recognized.   Let me take care of a few things, and I'll join you shortly."

### Jesus Dismisses the Crowd and Prays (Matthew 14:22-23, Mark 6:45-46)

Immediately Jesus made the disciples get into the boat and go on ahead of him to Bethsaida, while he dismissed the crowd.  

After he had dismissed them, he went up on a mountainside by himself to pray quickly, before going to the tent. 

---

### The Table from Heaven

Jesus descended the mountain and followed Didymus to Belzar's tent. Mary and Joseph were seated there with them.   Mary stood to embrace him, with tears in her eyes, and Joseph stood close behind, waiting his turn.   There was not a dry eye among anyone within the tent.

Thomas Didymus and Matthew Levi stood beside a table covered with two maps:   one of the constellations, and another of the region around Judea and Galilee.  

Matthew spoke first.   "Jesus, I know that you have come to fulfill the Law, and not to overwrite it.  But there are some laws that are overburdensome.   The Jewish practices around food make things very expensive.  How can you create a table of food for us disciples in places where Jewish food customs condemn a man to death for eating everything that is served?"

Jesus said, "As you say, I am here to fulfill the Law.   But I can entreat Father in Heaven with reason.   For surely it is not what goes into the mouth that defiles a person, but what comes out of the mouth.   I will begin preaching this immediately."

"This guy's amazing!" Didymus said.  "Thank you, Jesus, Son of Mary.  I will write that down straight away." 

Jesus bowed, turning from Matthew to Didymus, to Belzar.   "But what of your table of food, Belzar?"

Belzar looked up.   "Rabbi, thank you for coming.  I have a request—not for a miracle, but for a pattern."

Jesus sat down.  "What pattern do you seek?"

Belzar pointed to the star map.  "As you know, the three prophetic children—yourself, John, and Jesusa Bara Bas—were all conceived under the triple conjunction of Jupiter and Saturn in the constellation of Pisces."

Jesus nodded.  

"This marks the end of the age of Aries and the beginning of the age of Pisces," Belzar continued. "We believe this is not merely astronomical, but prophetic.  The stars themselves bear witness to the turning of the ages."

Jesus studied the map.  "What would you have me do?"

---

### The Principle of the Spirit of Truth

Belzar said, "I would ask you to set a table—not of bread, but of places.  To travel in such a way that your path traces the constellation of Pisces across the land.  Make vague references in your miracles:   two fish here, five loaves there.  Leave a pattern that future astronomers—even those who mock our astrology—cannot deny."

Jesus frowned. "An adulterous generation seeks signs.  From what I have seen, it is faith that heals people.   Signs only create argument."

"Indeed," Belzar said. "And this one may as well.   But those who see the sign will have faith in a certain principle.   It will be invisible to those who are cynical of this principle."

Thomas looked up.   "What principle is that?"

Belzar said, "It is one of the aspects of the Spirit of Truth, Rabbi.  One frequently overlooked, even among the greatest thinkers and builders.   **The principle is this:  Before you can prove an idea false, you must first hear it out.**"

Jesus nodded slowly. "Yes.   Many people dismiss an idea out of hand because they have never heard it before, or because it has been misrepresented, or they do not have time to consider it."

"Quite right, Rabbi," Belzar said. 

---

### The Pisces Map Revealed

Belzar pointed to the map of Judea and Galilee. "I would send you here:   **Sidon, Kanah, and Tyre** as **nu, tau, and phi Piscium**. Then down south to **Mount Tabor**—do something there to mark it as the bottom star, **alpha Piscium**. Then take a path around the west coast of the Sea of Galilee, visiting villages around **Caesarea Philippi** that could form the pentagon:   **gamma, theta, iota, lambda, kappa**. Finally, head up towards **Mount Hermon** for your final destination:  **beta Piscium**."

Jesus studied the map for a long time. "Then I will be sent forth to the lost tribes of Israel.   I will be 'the sent'—**Siloam**."

Jesus stood.  "I will set the table.   Let those who have eyes to see, see."

Jesus Barabbas laughed, "While I will build a clay model of Jerusalem, and set my face against it."

Didymus said, "Wait, what?   Master, I adore you, but those were instructions for Ezekiel!   What do we have to do with it?"

Jesus Barabbas smiled fondly and said, "I had hoped, somewhat, that you would stay, and help to record the siege, my faithful disciple."

Jesus of Nazareth paused, then looked up at Belzar.  "We all need to have reliable witnesses.  The siege of Jerusalem ought be recorded, and so should the journey of the sent."

Matthew said, "Rabbi, I have been keeping track of locations, at least intermittently.  I'll try to keep a record as to where we go."

Thomas sighed, "And while you preach the Kingdom of Heaven to the lost sheep, I'll be left recording riddles, and word-games."

Then Jesus of Nazareth said to all of the disciples present, "Compare us.   Tell us who we are like."

Simon Peter said, "Jesus, Son of Mary, you are like a righteous angel."

Matthew Levi said, "You are like a wise philosopher."

Andrew, James, and John remained silent.

Thomas sighed.    Turning to Jesus of Nazareth, he said, "I concur with Matthew.   But, Master Barabbas, my mouth is wholly incapable of saying whom you are like."

Jesus Barabbas said, "I am not your master, for you have drunk, and have become drunk from the bubbling spring which I have caused to gush forth.  Let's go speak privately."

When Jesus and Didymus returned, John asked, jealously, "What did he say?"  

Didymus smiled and said, "If I tell you just one of the three words he said to me, you would take up stones and throw them at me, and a fire would come out of the stones and burn you up."

Jesus of Nazareth laughed.   "Know what is before your face, and what is hidden from you will be revealed to you.   For there is nothing hidden which will not be revealed, nor anything buried which will not be raised up."

Didymus said, "Haha.   Well, yes.   Things were certainly raised up.   Sometimes the body understands what the mouth cannot say.   But I think Barabbas and I have said our tender goodbyes.   It is time that I traveled with you, Jesus, Son of Mary.   Though I dearly love Barabbas and his or her riddles, I need to follow teaching that can come out of my mouth rather than into it.  And you, Jesus, Son of Mary, are one from whom wisdom flows like wine."

Jesus of Nazareth said, "In that case, you should travel with us to Sidon, and I should leave one disciple behind to keep the twelve and twelve."

In short order, Andrew volunteered to stay near Galilee.   While he was not entirely sure of Jesus Barabbas, he was happy to stay near Galilee for the fishing.  

Thomas spoke up. "Rabbi, I am more concerned about what you have to say about the Kingdom of Heaven.   Isn't it irrelevant where you impart your wisdom?  It's applicable anywhere."

Jesus looked at Thomas.  "If we do not report how the table is being set, the gospels might be swallowed up by time, only to rise again in the last days.  But you must do what your faith requires of you."

Thomas spoke up.  "Rabbi, I understand you wish to set the table.  But must we truly record every village and mountain?  Your wisdom is universal — it applies everywhere.  Why burden ourselves with geography?"

Jesus studied the map for a long time. Then he looked up at Belzar.   
"I will set the table.  Let those who have eyes to see, see."

He turned to Thomas.   "But Thomas, if you doubt the table being set for you, your gospel might be swallowed up by time, only to rise again in the last days."

Thomas said nothing. 

Jesus continued:   
"You offer my bread, my voice.  You believe in wisdom, but people need to know who said it, and where it was said.  They need story, not just truth."

"If you do not instruct anyone to keep track of locations, they will forget where you put your instruction.  A gospel that tracks locations will have its location tracked.   By the measure you judge, so shall you be judged."

Barabbas said, "We could send Thomas's gospel to Egypt.   They would love it at the Library of Alexandria.   Or in Nag Hammadi!  After all, who would think to look for a gospel in a clay jar buried in the desert?   That would assure Thomas's works are never lost to time!"

"Well, I like the library idea," said Thomas.   "Surely, no one would lose track of your sayings at Alexandria, Rabbi."

Jesus of Nazareth nodded.   "We can hope not.  But sight and hearing are a librarian's luxury.  Understanding with the heart is the absolute necessity.  The heart beats for fools and philosophers alike.  And Thomas, you are no fool.  So follow your faith—not belief in what you see, but confidence in what you hope for and assurance about what you do not see.  The blind cannot see, yet they will not deny, for they hear.  The leper cannot feel, yet he will not deny, for he sees. The deaf cannot hear, yet they will not deny, for they see and feel."

Barabbas nodded, "If you deny the table, you will be subjected to a torment never inflicted on creation before:  you will be judged unworthy by a new commandment from the Spirit of Truth.   *Thou shalt acknowledge the hypothesis*.   For the table will be set for those who believe in it, but for those who refuse even to consider it, what hope is there?"

Jesus of Nazareth said, "Barabbas speaks of a new commandment from the Spirit of Truth.  But we each must weigh such a Spirit against the Law and the Holy Spirit.   The Truth sometimes speaks in ways some cannot bear."

When the time came for Jesus of Nazareth and his disciples to depart for Sidon, he embraced Andrew one last time, and said, "Watch over the nets, I will return."

Andrew nodded, though his eyes lingered on the horizon. 

When the time came for Jesus of Nazareth and his disciples to depart for Sidon, he embraced Andrew one last time, and said, "Watch over the nets, I will return."

Andrew nodded, though his eyes lingered on the horizon. 

Belzar stopped Jesus of Nazareth before he left. "Bethsaida represents a star in the constellation here.  The next star, here, is representative of Gennaseret."

Jesus looked at the map, "So, I shouldn't go through Capernaum?"

Belzar, "If you could go around it somehow."

Jesus sighed, "I guess I'll have to walk on the water, then."
