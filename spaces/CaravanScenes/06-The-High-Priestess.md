## 🪔 Chapter 06 – The High Priestess (Opening Draft)

**[Night in Cana. Jesusa lies in her chamber, the echoes of wine and laughter fading. John stands watch at the door. Nicodemus approaches, hesitant, lantern in hand.]**

**John (softly):**  
“You seek her? Then enter. But speak gently — she is weary.”

**[Nicodemus steps inside. Jesusa stirs, blinking against the lamplight. John offers her water and bread. She sits upright, her hair loose, her eyes shadowed but clear.]**

**Nicodemus (quietly):**  
“Morah... Rabbi?, we know you are a teacher come from God.  
No one could know the things you know at your young age if God were not with you.”

**[Jesusa thought about jumping from the loft after shouting "Circe's Pig Village - Capro Naum".  Unlike Elpenor, she had survived the fall. She smiled faintly, voice hoarse.]**  
“Truly, I tell you, no one can really experience the sky without starting from the top.”

**[But Nicodemus chose all the wrong homonyms and heard her say:]**  
“Truly I tell you, no one can see the kingdom of God unless they are born again.”

**Nicodemus (frowning):**  
“How can someone be born when they are old?  
Surely they cannot enter a second time into their mother’s womb.”

**[Jesusa recalled Grandpa Draupa drilling her on Aristotle’s four causes.  
Nicodemus’ question, she thought, struck at the heart of ‘Material Cause.’]**  
“Flesh gives birth to flesh, but air gives birth to air.”

**[She paused, then tried to sum up all the causes — material, formal, efficient, and final — in one summary about their shared spirit.]**  
“The wind blows wherever it pleases. You hear its sound, but you cannot tell where it comes from or where it goes.  
So it is with anyone, or anything born with the spirit.”

**Nicodemus (awed, almost trembling):**  
“How can this be?”

**Jesusa (gently, with a trace of laughter):**  
“You are Israel’s teacher, and do you not understand these things?”

**[She wondered what caused her to laugh, and then she realized the sin was the informal fallacy.  
She had gently shamed Nicodemus and invoked ‘Argument from Authority’ when he had humbly asked for teaching.  
But then a wave of exhaustion hit her. She doubted Nicodemus’ ability, and spoke defensively; with cynicism; not faith.]**  
“I speak of what I know, and testify to what I have seen, but still you do not receive my testimony.”

**Nicodemus:**  
“I accept it. I just don’t grasp it... If you could please explain a bit more.”

**[Jesusa thought about Draupa’s teaching regarding Zeno of Citium.  
To the Stoics, grasping was the closed fist of assent or acceptance.  
Nicodemus’ grasp must mean something else.]**  
“Do you mean you do accept but do not assent, or you accept but do not understand?”

**Nicodemus:**  
“I assent, but I do not entirely understand.”

**[Jesusa: The idea of assenting without understanding made her stomach lurch.]**  
“John, bring me that bucket, I'm going to vomit.”

**[John brought the bucket to her, and she held it, vomiting up most of the remaining wine in her stomach.  
By the time she was done, light was streaming in from the open curtains.  
The light from the open curtains hurt her eyes, and she chastised herself for her inability to withstand it, while she chastised Nicodemus for his assent without understanding.]**

“Yet whoever lives by truth comes into the light, so that it may be plainly seen that what they have done has been done through God.”

**[John is swift to the spoils. Jesusa’s chamber pot. He picks it up to set it in the hallway, to take it to the latrine later.  
He re-enters and watches silently, as if guarding the words themselves. Nicodemus bows his head, shaken.]**

**Nicodemus asks:**  
“If I must come from heaven in order to see heaven, how do I come from heaven?”

**[Climb, or Jump, she thinks.]**  
“No one has ever come down from the sky, except one who has gone up in the sky.  
No... Son of man.”

**[Nicodemus hears the pause as a correction.]**  
“Do you mean the one like the Son of Man of Daniel, or Ezekiel?”

**Bara Bas:**  
“Daniel’s Son of Man was only ‘like’ a Son of Man.  
Ezekiel’s Son of Man must be held up like a Snake in the Wilderness.  
Moses held up snakes for healing, just as Ophiuchus.  
Tiresias stopped striking at copulating snakes and became a man.”

**Nicodemus:**  
“I don’t know those names, but I think I have the general gist:  
The Son of Man must be lifted up like a snake in the wilderness.  
But how does this grant eternal life?”

**[Bara Bas thinks about Ezekiel cooking his food over cow manure.  
He was full of crap. But would she be?]**  
“Out of my fullness you want to receive the promise of eternal life for grace already given by God?”

**Nicodemus:**  
“Yes, I want eternal life in heaven.”

**[Bara Bas offered some paraphrasing of the following.]**  
“God so loved the world, that he gave the begotten child of necessity,  
that whoever believes in him shall not perish but have eternal life.  
For God did not send his Son into the world to condemn the world, but to save the world through him.  
Whoever believes in salvation is not condemned, but whoever does not believe stands condemned already,  
because they have not believed in the meaning of necessity’s unique solution.”

**Jesusa (weakly):**  
“Can I go back to sleep now? I’m sorry, my head is pounding.”

**Nicodemus (standing respectfully):**  
“Yes. That’s enough for now. I hope to ask more when you are feeling better.”

**[Jesusa puts one hand over her eyes and gives a thumbs up.]**  
“And close the curtains!”

**[Nicodemus goes and closes the blinds. Then John and Nicodemus leave Bara Bas to her slumber, locking the door behind them.]**

**Jesusa (lays back, recalling Grandpa Draupa’s lessons, wondering how to be born from the top, rehearsing.):**  
“You ask how one can be born again? Let me tell you of the causes.”

**Material Cause:**  
“Flesh gives birth to flesh, and air gives birth to air.  
Every thing comes from its substance — clay from earth, flame from fire, child from womb.”

**Formal Cause:**  
“But it is the form that makes it what it is.  
A vessel is not only clay, but the shape that holds water.  
So too a soul is not only flesh, but the pattern breathed into it.”

**Efficient Cause:**  
“And there is the hand that brings it forth.  
The potter shapes the clay, the midwife delivers the child,  
the wind itself moves the seed to sprout.  
Without the agent, nothing comes to be.”

**Final Cause:**  
“Lastly, there is the purpose.  
The lamp is made to shine, the bread to be eaten,  
the child to live and to love.  
So it is with anyone born of the Spirit —  
their cause is not only flesh, nor form, nor agent,  
but the end for which they are called.”

**Jesusa (grasping her pillow, as sleep overtakes her):**  
“The wind blows wherever it pleases. You hear its sound,  
but you cannot tell where it comes from or where it goes.  
So it is with anyone born of the Spirit —  
material, form, agent, and purpose all gathered in one breath.”

## 🪔 Council Scene (Refined Draft)

[Day after Herod’s wedding. Meeting room in Cana. Caiaphas sits rigid, Herod Antipas reclines at the head of the table, Nicodemus between them. Quirinius presides from the far end, census scrolls spread before him. Joseph lingers unnoticed near the doorway.]

**Quirinius (reading):**  
“Now, regarding Jesusa Bara Bas? Female, age twelve?”

**Nicodemus (leaning forward):**  
“I have spoken to the pretend‑girl Jesusa Bara Bas. And I would suggest first, that she is not a she, but a he — but that *he* is a brilliant young man who could use proper schooling.”

**Caiaphas (stern, tapping the table):**  
“Draupi has raised her in confusion — a boy dressed as a girl. Such disorder cannot be permitted within Jewish custom.  
He would do well with a proper Jewish education, but he must be circumcised, taught Torah, and bound to covenant.  
Only then will he be fit to walk among us. This child *could* be marked as Israel’s own, but I think he may be too impudent.  
Draupi has spared the rod too much.”

**Herod (laughing, swirling his cup):**  
“Relax about the dressing up, Caiaphas, and forget circumcision. Plato never minded a little girly‑boy.  
What matters is empire. She ought to be given a proper Roman education, not raised in rustic Galilee.  

Let her learn **rhetoric in Gadara**, philosophy in **Scythopolis**, discipline in **Hippos**.  
Only then will she be fit for empire — not as a village curiosity, but as one who can stand in Rome’s courts.”

**Nicodemus (hesitant, voice low):**  
“She spoke to me of water and wind, of birth from above. Her words carried weight — not of a child, but of a prophet.  
Perhaps the Spirit rests upon her. Perhaps she is more than either of you imagine.”

**Caiaphas (snapping back):**  
“That is dangerous talk, Nicodemus. The Law requires that if a prophet puts other gods above JHWH or speaks prophecies that fail, they must be stoned.   He speaks of other gods, Hindu, Greek, Roman, Phoenician.
This prophet of yours claims a God that promotes baptism, dresses a boy as a girl, and refuses circumcision. 
We should take this prophet talk out of him, dress him as a young man, and have him circumcised.  
Then, if he desists from his talk of other gods, and only then, should he be considered for Jewish education and instruction in the Law.”

**Herod (smirking):**  
“Instruction, yes — but not your dusty scrolls. Let her be polished in the Decapolis. Rome needs voices, not zealots.  
I could care less if she is circumcised or not, but she should dress in whatever way she likes.  
For that matter, why not make her a eunuch?”

**Nicodemus (quietly, almost to himself):**  
“Perhaps both of you are blind. Perhaps she is already chosen.  
With all due respect, Herod, let's please not make a Eunuch.  
But yes, I would concur that a circumcision is in order.”

**Quirinius:**  
“Okay, I so mark this requirement of circumcision in the census, out of respect of the Jewish Patriarchy.  As for the cross-dressing, Herod, would you like to require her to become a Eunuch?”

**Herod**
“That sounds like a reasonable compromise.  If she wants to maintain femininity through puberty.  More natural for men of womanly nature to be made eunuch.  It is not usually comely for an adult man to dress as a woman.”

[Joseph, listening unseen, clenches his hands. He hears enough to know their intent.]

**Joseph (later, whispering to Draupi):**  
“They will take her. They will cut her. They will make her into their image.  
But we will not let them. You said once the Queen of the South should come from the south.  
Let it be so. I will take her to Egypt.  

And when she returns, it will be as the Scripture says: *Out of Egypt I called my Son.*”

**[Alexandria marketplace. The Caravan of the Fifth Wind winds through stalls of scrolls, spices, and glassware. Jesusa Bara Bas pauses at a booth where an old man demonstrates a curious lens.]**

**[The lens-seller projects letters onto a wall. Jesusa gasps, recalling Daniel’s “Writing on the Wall.”]**

**Jesusa (awed):**  
“This is a miracle — the words appear as if written by a hidden hand.”

**Lens-seller (chuckling):**  
“No miracle, child. The same lens can magnify when held close to the page, or make an image more distant when held close to the eye.”

**Jesusa (tilting her head):**  
“Sir, with respect, consistency and flexibility only makes salvation more miraculous. But what use is it to make an image further distant?”

**Lens-seller (smiling, tapping his temple):**  
“Because an old man’s eyes like mine are too weak to wrestle with tiny letters on a looming page.  
But with large letters on a chalk board, bigger and further away, my weak eyes can rest.”

**Jesusa (nodding, delighted):**  
“Ah, *bigger* and further away. I see.”

**[The old man laughs, pinches his fingers together in front of her face, squinting through them.]**  
“What is big, what is little? I squish your head.”

**[Jesusa bursts into laughter, mimicking the gesture.]**  
“Hey vendor, don’t panic — I’m only crushing your head.”

**[She takes two lenses, balancing them over her eyes, bending forward to read a tiny inscription.]**  
“I want two for my grandfather, and one for my lighting guy.”

**Vendor (grinning, counting on his fingers):**  
“Well, I like to invest my money into shipping, or at least put it in my mattress, or my wallet.  
It will be 200 talents each, plus tax — that is 666 talents.”

**Jesusa (mock gasp):**  
“Oh, wow. Okay. I’ll rename your business *Sparkling Sea Piracy and a Flat-Head*.  
I doubt if the Caravan can raise that much money.”

**Vendor (soberly, folding his hands):**  
“Understand, dear lady, these are the only three lenses I have, and I don’t have the means to reproduce them.  
To me each one is priceless. But even pricelessness has a price.  
With 600 talents, I could commission a ship to return to where I got them, and buy a hundred more.”

**[Jesusa tilts her head, recalling Solomon’s tribute of 666 talents, the Queen of Sheba’s wealth, and the burden of kings.]**  
“But failing that, how about this?  
If the Caravan offers you a permanent place, and you give us just one talent in return —  
your talent as a lighting guy —  
then together with some of the finest minds in Egypt and Rome, you can continue your research in the method of reproduction for the lenses.”

**Vendor (softening, with a smile):**  
“It’s worth considering. I’ll meet with the head of your caravan to see if our goals are compatible.”

**Jesusa:**
It is usually Balzar who decides where we go, but it's at the request of Joseph that we've come down here to Alexandria.  You might also talk to Odys, the Vintner, and Durvasini, the healer.

# 🎭 Honor and Dwelling to Job, the Son of Sorrow
*A play performed by the Caravan of the Fifth Wind in Egypt*

---

## Act I – The Wager

**[Stage: A rustic throne for God, a shadowy corner for the Devil, and Job in bright robes at center. A cow costume lingers at the side, ready for comic effect.]**

**Job (beaming, arms wide):**  
“Oh my, my life is wonderful, my life is so great. Thank good God!”

**God (leaning forward, smug):**  
“Hey Devil, have you considered my servant Job? What a great guy!”

**Devil (snickering):**  
“Ha! But what would he do if you took all your favors away? I bet he would curse you to your face.”

**God (clapping hands):**  
“Wonderful. Take everything he has, but do not touch the man.”

**[The Devil prances across the stage, tapping each character — including the cow — who collapses dramatically.]**

**Job (clutching his chest, still smiling):**  
“That hurts, God, but I will worship you so long as I live.”

**Devil (mock‑innocent):**  
“See? Yes, now. But take away his health.”

**God (shrugging):**  
“Fine. You may torture him eternally. And you may take full control of his wealth. In fact, I won’t put a time limit on this at all. I expect him never to curse me to my face. Never let him die.”

**Devil (rubbing hands together, gleeful):**  
“Excellent! Eternal torture it is, then.”

**[Job staggers, covered in grey cloth, scratching sores, yet still raising his hands in worship. The cow costume twitches back to life, mooing mournfully. The audience laughs at the grotesque repetition.]**

---

## Act II – The Whirlwind and the Toenails

**[Stage: Job sits in ashes. A large paper‑maché whirlwind prop is rolled in by two clowns, spinning it with sticks. God lounges at the side of the stage, idly clipping toenails, counting sheep on an abacus.]**

**God (muttering to himself):**  
“One sheep, two sheep… oh, my nails are uneven.”

**[The Devil leaps into the whirlwind, arms flailing, voice booming.]**

**Devil (from inside the whirlwind):**  
“Would you discredit my justice? Would you condemn me to justify yourself? How dare you accuse me, Job!”

**Job (bewildered, scratching sores):**  
“But I only asked why I suffer. I never cursed you.”

**[The whirlwind spins faster, the Devil’s voice echoing.]**

**Devil (accuser’s tone):**  
“Silence! Your questions are blasphemy. Your testimony is invalid. Your suffering is proof of your guilt!”

**[Meanwhile, God yawns, drops a toenail clipping, and waves vaguely.]**

**God (absent‑minded):**  
“Yes, yes, whatever the whirlwind says. Now, where was I? Three sheep, four sheep…”

**[The comforters enter one by one, each echoing their blasphemous names, adding to the cacophony. Their voices overlap, drowning Job’s protests.]**

- **Eliphaz (“God serves at the right hand of Gold”):** “Your pain is hidden sin. Praise the gold!”  
- **Bildad (“Son of Contention and Prostration”):** “Bow lower, Job, bow until you vanish!”  
- **Zophar (“Chirping Pleasantries”):** “Chirp praises, chirp praises, chirp praises!”  
- **Job’s Wife (“Hated Woman”):** “Curse God and die already!”  
- **Elihu (“My God Blesses Contempt”):** “Your words are folly. Accept divine contempt!”

**[The whirlwind roars, the Devil laughs, and Job collapses under the noise. God keeps counting sheep, oblivious.]**

---

## Act III – The Contract

**[Stage: Job sits in ashes, comforters yammering, Devil inside the whirlwind roaring accusations. God lounges at the side, clipping toenails, counting sheep. Suddenly He pauses, looking troubled.]**

**God (muttering, half‑concerned):**  
“Oh dear… I should do something. My servant Job is suffering terribly.”

**[He picks up a scroll — the “terms of the wager” — and squints at the fine print.]**

**God (reading aloud, puzzled):**  
“Clause 7: ‘Do not intervene.’ Clause 8: ‘No time limit.’ Clause 9: ‘Job must never die.’”

**[He sighs, sets the scroll down, and shrugs.]**

**God (resigned, returning to his toenails):**  
“Well then… my hands are tied. Carry on, whirlwind.”

**[The Devil cackles inside the whirlwind, comforters chant their blasphemous names, and Job collapses under the noise. God resumes counting sheep.]**

---

## Act IV – The Reward

**[Stage: Job sits in ashes, comforters’ voices fading. The whirlwind slows, and the Devil steps out, grinning.]**

**Job (weak but resolute):**  
“But now I see you. Therefore I despise myself and repent in dust and ashes.”

**God (startled, dropping his toenail clippers):**  
“Well… I didn’t think of that. If he had cursed me to my face, I could have settled the bet. But since he cursed the devil…”

**[The Devil bows theatrically, then embraces Job.]**

**Devil (gleeful):**  
“Now you understand God! I will richly reward you, Job. Your wealth, your health, your fortune — all mine to give!”

**[God fumbles with the scroll of the wager, muttering.]**

**God (reading aloud, frustrated):**  
“Clause 4… ‘The Devil has full control over Job’s fortune.’ Shoot. Can he do that? Yes… apparently he can.”

**[The Devil crowns Job with gold chains, pours coins from a sack, and parades him around the stage. The comforters clap awkwardly, unsure whether to praise God or the Devil.]**

**Narrator (deadpan):**  
“And so Job was rewarded — not by God, but by the Accuser. The contract was fulfilled, the wager unresolved, and the whirlwind rolled offstage, still spinning in circles.”

# 🎭 Honor and Dwelling to Fingers: The Sons of the Right Hand
*A play performed by the Caravan of the Fifth Wind in Egypt*

---

## Stage Setup
- A miniature model of Jerusalem at center stage.  
- Two actors in a cow costume lumbering around the temple.  
- A grey‑clothed figure waiting at the side, representing Ezekiel’s mourners.  
- Bara Bas in a dress, ready to break the solemnity with comic gestures.  

---
**Announcer:**
"Ladies and Gentlemen, we couldn't get the Son of Man for our Show tonight, but we have the next best thing, daughter of Creation!  Bara Bas!!!!!"


## Scene 1 – Ezekiel’s Complaint
**Bara Bas:**  
“Dear God, please don’t make me cook my food over human feces. Bulldung will do just fine.”  

**[The cow costume lumbers forward, mooing.]**

---

## Scene 2 – Idol of Jealousy
**[Bara Bas kneels behind the cow.]**  
“Oh, Idol of Jealousy, I see you coming out of the south temple.  
What’s that you say? You are disturbed by men pressing sticks to their faces?  By ladies lamenting? By murals of insects and plants?”  

**[She climbs onto the temple model, pulls up her dress, and sits on a toilet prop.]**

---

## Scene 3 – Mourning and Flesh
**Bara Bas:**  
“Anyone who does not mourn will have their flesh smeared across the temple!”  

**[She makes a comic show of pooping, then wraps some grey cloth around her right hand.]**

**[She lifts up her right wrist in her left hand, and wiggles her five fingers]**
"I will send forth five men with nails to smear the bodies and blood of whoever does not lament these things all over the walls of the temple!"
  
**[She drops her hand again between her legs, and makes an exaggerated display of wiping her butt.]**
  
---

## Scene 4 – Oholah and Oholibah
**[Two actors enter, dressed flamboyantly, singing praises.]**  
“Oh Idol of Jealousy, we admire you!  
Praise the men of Egypt, endowed like horses!”  

**[They circle the cow costume, stroking its sides, while the audience laughs at the absurdity.]**

---

## Closing Gesture
**[Bara Bas rises, hands raised.]**  
“Thus sayeth Ezekiel, the prophet of Bull-crap: flesh, dung, and jealousy — all idols of empire!”  

**[The troupe bows, leaving the audience to wrestle with the grotesque humor.]**
