## Part 10: The Hierophant (John 5, ~30 CE)

### John's Recovery at Bethesda

As John recovered from the poison, they moved him once again, this time to the healing waters of Bethesda. Herodias and Salome came as well, and Draupi joined them. 

Salome's eyes scanned the courtyard, and she stopped suddenly. "That man is no cripple," she said, pointing to a man lying on a mat near the pool.

Barabbas approached him and asked, "Do you desire to be well?"

The man replied, "I have no one to help me get down to the water."

Barabbas returned to Salome and said, "I don't know if he can walk, but he is lonely."

Salome said, "Make him walk."

Barabbas returned to the man, saying, "Pick up your mat and walk."

The man, appearing quite unsteady, got up on both feet and rolled up the mat, using it as a cane.  Barabbas left again, so he, Salome, and John could watch from a distance.

The man stood for a while, then made a feeble step forward. 

An attendant rushed forward.  "What are you doing? You're going to fall!"

He said, "There was a man here. He told me to pick up my mat and walk."

The attendant said, "It's Sunday. There's no one here to help you if you fall."

He looked around, crestfallen. *No one here. * He sighed. "I won't fall."

Then he dropped the mat and walked solemnly out of Bethesda.

---

### The Sphinx's Riddle

Barabbas then said, "What has no legs, then three legs, then two?"

Salome said, "No, when the Sphinx asked Oedipus the riddle it was more 'I have four legs in the morning, two legs at noon, and three legs at night.'"

Barabbas said, "Right. More of a 'what am I' riddle than a 'what is it' riddle."

Salome said, "Yeah, but I got it.  No legs—the guy laying on his mat. Three legs—the guy using his mat for a cane. Two legs—the guy walking out of here."

Herodias raised an eyebrow. "So you two are Queen of Sheba and King Solomon but gender-swapped."

Barabbas considered.  "No. Not the Queen of Sheba. My mother prayed directly to Typhon so that she would bear the Sphinx."

Herodias said, "And I prayed Salome would have the wisdom of Solomon."

At that moment, the attendant came to them, saying, "Did you tell that man to walk?"

Jesus said, "Yes."

The attendant said, "Please, do not bother anyone else.  This is Sunday, and we don't have attendants around in case anyone should fall. Also, the ill are lined up fairly, so that when the waters are stirred by an angel, the first one in line should go."

Jesus said, "The Jewish JHWH rested on the Seventh day, but my father does not. My Father is always at his work to this very day, and I too am working."

The attendant said, "Your father?  Is he the one who just walked out of here? You want to make him equal to God?"

Jesus said, "Very truly I tell you, the Son can do nothing by himself; he can do only what he sees his Father doing, because whatever the Father does the Son also does."

The attendant crosses his arms, looking down, seeing John lying there, vaguely aware of Jesus continued droning.  

Salome nodded, saying quietly to Herodias, "That's a clever twist on the 'what am I' riddle.  Typhon is a hurricane."

---

### Barabbas's Teaching (John 5:19–47)

Jesus continued, "For the Father loves the Son and shows him all he does. Yes, and he will show him even greater works than these, so that you will be amazed."

Salome nodded, whispering, "I'm amazed. The Sphinx's riddles never had any 'father/son' part to them."

Jesus continued:  "For just as the Father raises the dead and gives them life, even so the Son gives life to whom he is pleased to give it."

Salome asked, "Did Typhon ever raise the dead?"

Herodias (or Draupi) said, "Typhon is the storm that brings life after drought. He raises the land from death."

The attendant looks closer at John, thinking, *he's not dead.  He's just sleeping.  Might have a fever.*

Jesus said, "Moreover, the Father judges no one, but has entrusted all judgment to the Son."

Salome said, "Typhon judged no one, but the Sphinx judged everyone before Oedipus."

Jesus said, "That all may honor the Son just as they honor the Father."

Salome said, "That all may honor the Sphinx just as they honor the hurricane."

Jesus said, "Whoever does not honor the Son does not honor the Father, who sent him."

Salome said, "Whoever does not honor the Sphinx does not honor the Typhoon that sent her."

Jesus said, "Very truly I tell you, whoever hears my word and believes him who sent me has eternal life and will not be judged but has crossed over from death to life."

The attendant raises an eyebrow, thinking *Oh, another one of those eternal life crackpots.*

Salome said, "Whoever takes riddles to solve from the entropy that is inevitable will escape the laws of entropy and cross from death to eternal life."

Jesus said, "Very truly I tell you, a time is coming and has now come when the dead will hear the voice of the Son of God and those who hear will live."

Bored with the monolog, the attendant rolls his eyes and leaves.  

Salome said, "A time is coming when souls bound for Sheol will hear the voice of Salvation, and those who hear will live."

Jesus said, "For as the Father has life in himself, so he has granted the Son also to have life in himself."

Salome said, "So as the Typhoon has life, he has granted the Sphinx life."

Jesus said, "And he has given him authority to judge because he is the Son of Man."

Salome said, "The Sphinx is Bar Abba—Son of the Father; Son of Man."

Jesus said, "Do not be amazed at this, for a time is coming when all who are in their graves will hear his voice and come out—those who have done what is good will rise to live, and those who have done what is evil will rise to be condemned."

Salome said, "The hurricane will blow the bodies out of their graves. But those who have sought good in the future will rise to live. Those who have not sought good will be erased by entropy."

Jesus said, "By myself I can do nothing; I judge only as I hear, and my judgment is just, for I seek not to please myself but him who sent me."

Salome asked, "A riddler judges an answer not to please himself, but the riddle."

Jesus said, "If I testify about myself, my testimony is not true."

Salome said, "If this riddle is about Barabbas, the testimony is not true."

Jesus said, "There is another who testifies in my favor, and I know that his testimony about me is true."

Salome said, "I testify in Barabbas's favor, and he knows my testimony is true."

Jesus said, "You have sent to John and he has testified to the truth."

Salome said, "I sent for John's head, and he says I'm an inbred."

Jesus said, "Not that I accept human testimony; but I mention it that you may be saved."

Salome said, "Whatever."

Jesus said, "John was a lamp that burned and gave light, and you chose for a time to enjoy his light."

Salome said, "He'll get better."

Jesus said, "I have testimony weightier than that of John. For the works that the Father has given me to finish—the very works that I am doing—testify that the Father has sent me."

Salome said, "You've been sent by the Typhoon."

Jesus said, "And the Father who sent me has himself testified concerning me.  You have never heard his voice nor seen his form, nor does his word dwell in you, for you do not believe the one he sent."

Salome said, "This attendant has no idea about the Sphinx or Typhon."

Jesus said, "You study the Scriptures diligently because you think that in them you have eternal life. These are the very Scriptures that testify about me."

Salome said, "I wonder why no one goes to Isaiah 28:15 to have life?"

Jesus said, "Yet you refuse to come to me to have life."

Salome said, "Right?"

Jesus said, "I do not accept glory from human beings, but I know you. I know that you do not have the love of God in your hearts.  I have come in my Father's name."

Salome said, "As in Bara? Creation? Or as in Jesus? Salvation? I don't see any way you've come in Typhon's name."

Jesus said, "And you do not accept me; but if someone else comes in his own name, you will accept him."
Salome said, "People will accept Jesus of Nazareth.  And for that matter, anybody that doesn't talk in 'What am I' riddles."

"How can you believe since you accept glory from one another but do not seek the glory that comes from the only God?"
Salome said "People would rather have community than truth."

Jesus said, "But do not think I will accuse you before the Father.  Your accuser is Moses, on whom your hopes are set."
Salome said, "Moses decided who must be stoned to death.  Good faith effort, but now you're stuck with his mistakes."

"If you believed Moses, you would believe me, for he wrote about me."
Salome said, "Are you the scapegoat?  The scapegoat wanders the wilderness with all the sins of Israel confessed on his head."

Jesus said, "But since you do not believe what he wrote, how are you going to believe what I say?"

### Herodias's Question

Herodias is very happy, based on Jesus response to the Pharisees and what Salome has whispered in her ear.

She says to Barabbas, "You are the Sphinx, who asks the riddles, and the judge to who gets them right.  My Salome, though, has the wisdom of Solomon, answering every question from the Queen of Sheba, as Oedipus answered the riddle of the sphinx."  

Barabbas answers, "Then Salome should receive many talents from me.  Is 666 per year sufficient?"

Salome says, "How about, instead, I'll give you my two cents, once a day.  That adds up to a little more; what 730 cents per year?"

Barabbas smiled, "I'm sure you'll gather many talents this way.  Your two cents are worth far more than riches."

Herodias says, "So your role is the Sphinx, then."

Barabbas says, "Perhaps.  But did the Sphinx deliver judgment?  I like to leave things more unsettled than that.  Tell my riddles and let the spirit take you where it may.  The wind blows where it wants to.  No one knows where it came from or where it is going."

Herodias says "Then let it be the wind should forever blow us toward God's Kingdom on Earth."

---

### Word to Jesus of Nazareth

At about one in the afternoon, John's fever left him.  He arose, now having heard how Barabbas had won over the people in Sychar.  He had also been listening to Jesus speak to the attendant at Bethesda, and Salome's whispers.  

He arose and said, "I'm feeling better, but barely myself."  He rubs his beard.  "I would like to go to the temple to pray."

When they got there, they found the man who had been lying at Bethesda.  He says to Jesus, "I tried to tell them that you had made me well, but you vanished.  What is your name?"

Jesus said, "  My name is Jesus.  I can see you are well.  Now, go.  Stop sinning, or someone worse may happen to you."

The man, not sure whether to take this as a threat said, 'okay!'  I will do right.  And I will tell them Jesus made me well.'"

Jesus said "Good, then your demons are leaving you.  Make sure to lock the door behind them so they can never come back in."

John wondered at this.  His childhood crush, now a man, casting out demons.  He knelt, praying at the temple, then came out, still rubbing his bare chin."  

"We should send someone to tell my father and mother that I'm alright," he said.  

"My brothers will go." said Barabbas.  "They've been feeling stir-crazy, and need a long walk."

---

Meanwhile, Jesus had left Cana, traveling slowly, as many mourners joined with him on his way to the western shore of Galilee.  By the time he arrived, around 5000 men and their wives and children were with him.

John came to Jesus at night, keeping his face in the shadows, in case someone might identify him because of his kind eyes.  When John met with him, he asked, "Jesus, there is another casting out demons in your name,"

Jesus said, "Do not stop him.  No one who casts out a demon in my name can in the next sentence say something against me.  Whoever is not against me is for me."

John said, "Then I shall follow Jesus Barabbas."
