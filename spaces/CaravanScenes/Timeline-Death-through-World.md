The sponge from the wine vinegar had earlier been applied to the bloody face of Jesus Barabbas.  So by putting that blood on the face of Jesus of Nazareth, two things happened.  (1) The door for the Christians was established.  Jesus of Nazareth becomes the doorframe marked by the blood of the lamb, which serves so that the God of the Jews can pass over them.  (2) Simultaneously, the Body of Jesus of Nazareth became the Temple of Poseidon requested of Odysseus by Tiresias in Homer's Odyssey.

Pilate had instructed the guards not to pierce Jesus' undergarment.  He described the undergarment as one that covered him from the top of his head to the ends of his toes.  The only undergarment they could find not to pierce, then, was his skin.  So they did not nail him to the cross.

The soldiers did not break Jesus’ legs, but a single witness has testified that he saw one of the soldiers pierce the side of Jesus of Nazareth. However, (Deut 17:6, 19:15, Mt 18:16) a single witness is not sufficient to indict that a crime has happened.

### The Sun
They returned to Jerusalem while Jesus of Nazareth went to Galilee, and eventually (in Acts) to Damascus.  When Ananias and Sapphira reported what they owned to Peter, he pretended that they died, and dispatched them to Damascus, to live with Jesus of Nazareth, who would later meet Saul of Tarsus, who became Paul. 
Spiritually speaking, the events fulfill a prophetic version of Leviticus 16; the Atonement ritual.  Jesus of Nazareth was the goat to be sacrificed for the Lord, while Jesus Barabbas was the goat to be set free in the wilderness with all the sins of Israel confessed over his head.  However, the roles were reversed in some sense, because Barabbas became the one actually crucified, and having eternal life (which, by the way, may be somewhat disqualifying as a Messiah).  Meanwhile Jesus of Nazareth lived an ordinary mortal life, having been a great teacher.  He died an ordinary life, as an ordinary man; suitable as a sacrifice to God.
