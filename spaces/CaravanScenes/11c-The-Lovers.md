## Part 3: The Lovers (~26 CE)

### The Mountain Contest (John 6:3-15)

**3** Then Jesus Barabbas went up on a mountainside and sat down with Salome, Herodias, Philip the Tetrarch, John (now the Apostle), Marcus (envoy of Caligula), and Andrew (brother of Simon Peter).  

**4** The Jewish Passover Festival had already started, and it was forbidden to eat *chametz* (leavened bread).

**5** When Jesus looked up and saw such important dignitaries coming toward him, he said to Philip, "Where shall we buy bread for these people to eat?"  

**6** He asked Philip about bread as money, and he already had a couple of ideas about bread in mind — what he was going to do.  

**7** Philip admired both Herodias and Salome, and answered him, "It ought cost two hundred denarii each to buy enough bread for each man to have a taste!"

**8** Another of his disciples, Andrew, Simon Peter's brother, spoke up, pointing to Pisces.    He pointed to the pentagon at one end, saying, "Here is a boy with five small barley loaves," and then to the other side where Pisces ends in two stars, "and two small fish."   He then gestured at all the stars.     "But how far will they go among so many?"

**10** Jesus said, "Have the people sit down."  

There was plenty of grass in that place, and Marcus opened a great chest.    He opened it and said, "Here are about five thousand men."  

He dumped the chest into the grass.    "Five thousand little round portraits of Caesar Philip, and others."  

**11** Jesus Barabbas then ran his hands through the money, spreading it out into a bed; distributed to the men who were seated as much as they wanted.   He did the same with the two women.    "Bread for the five loaves..." he said, "Bread for the two fish."

"Then let the contest begin," said Salome.

### Morning After the Contest

The morning sun cast long shadows across the hillside.    Five men lay scattered on the grass, exhausted but content.   Salome sat in the center, laughing as the night's events became the day's stories.

**12** "I don't know about you all," Philip said, "but this has made me very content."  

Jesus Barabbas said to his disciples, "Gather the pieces that are left over.     Let nothing be wasted."  

**13** So the five men searched around, gathering the coins and putting them back into chests and baskets.  

Marcus sat up, rubbing his eyes.    "I still can't believe Philip offered 200 denarii per person.    That's...    what, twelve hundred denarii for just us?"

Philip, still lying flat on his back, groaned.   "Don't remind me.   I thought I was being generous."

"You were," Salome said.    "But Marcus offered five thousand."

Marcus winced.   "Five thousand portraits of Caesar Philip, stamped in gold.   I thought that would be enough."

Barabbas laughed.   "It would have been.   But then I showed up with twelve handbaskets of yeast."

John frowned.   "How did you even *get* twelve handbaskets of yeast?"

Barabbas smiled.    "That will forever be my secret."

**14** Herodias said, "Surely this is the Prophet who is to come into the world."

Salome clapped her hands.   "And you won!    So now you're my first husband."

Barabbas's smile faded.   "Salome..."

"What?" She looked up at him, confused.  

"I can't," Barabbas said quietly.   "I won't."

Salome's face fell.   "But you won the contest."

"I won the *bread* contest," Barabbas said.   "And I'm honored.    But you're twelve.    And I'm...  not going to father a child with a twelve-year-old."

Herodias, who had been listening from a nearby tent, stepped forward.   "Barabbas, that's very noble of you.   But we need—"

"I know what you need," Barabbas interrupted.   "You need a plan to protect Salome from Caligula.   You need a child who can secure queenship.   But I'm not the one to give it to you, and it's certainly not the time."

John looked at Barabbas, then at Salome, then back at Barabbas.   "So...    what happens now?"

**15** Barabbas stood.    "Now I leave.    Before temptation causes me to make a king by force."

### The Boat Scene (John 6:16-21)

The others spent all day trying to account for every coin that had been spilled in the grass and frolicked on.     

Some of the money had already been spent: 
- Judas Iscariot had been paid handsomely for circumcising Marcus and Philip — and as always, Judas insisted much of it would go to the poor.
- A month's rent had been secured for the studio space in Capernaum where Jesus Barabbas was constructing his scale model of Jerusalem. 

**16** When evening came, they transported it all down to the lake,  

**17** where they got into a boat and set off across the lake for Capernaum.    By now it was dark, and Jesus had not yet joined them.  

**18** A strong wind was blowing and the waters grew rough.    

**19** When they had rowed about three or four miles, they saw Jesus approaching the boat.  

In the darkness, they thought they saw a man walking on the water; and they were frightened that it was someone who might steal their money.  

**20** But Jesus said to them, "It is I; don't be afraid."  

**21** Then they were willing to take him into the boat.    "No need," Jesus said, pulling the boat into the sand.    Immediately the boat reached the shore where they were heading.

### Herod's Investigation (John 6:22-24)

**22** The next day, Herod Antipas heard troubling rumors in Tiberias.  

His wife Herodias and her daughter Salome had been alone with five men in a single boat, and had traveled to Bethsaida.  What could they have been doing there?

He gathered his trackers and sent boats from Tiberias to investigate.   

One of his most skilled trackers arrived at the mountain where the contest had taken place.  He found evidence of a large gathering — grass trampled, remnants of bread, and coins scattered in the dirt.  

He followed the trail down to the shore and saw **one set of footprints in the sand**, leading north toward Capernaum.  Beside the footprints, there was clear evidence of a **heavy chest being dragged** into the boat.

The tracker reported back:  "One man walked alone.  The others took the boat with a great weight."

Herod's eyes narrowed.  "Who walks when a boat is waiting?"

The crowd that had stayed on the opposite shore of the lake realized that only one boat had been there, and that Jesus had not entered it with his disciples, but that they had gone away alone.  

**23** Then some boats from Tiberias landed near the place where the people had eaten the bread after the Lord had given thanks.  

**24** Once the crowd realized that neither Jesus nor his disciples were there, they got into the boats and went to Capernaum in search of Jesus.
