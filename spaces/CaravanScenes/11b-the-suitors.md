## Part 2: The Suitors (~26 CE)

### Marcus Aelius Varro Arrives in Tiberias

Meanwhile, Herodias and Salome had returned to Tiberias.  Herod was hosting an important envoy from Rome. 

"Herodias," Herod said, "this is Marcus.  Marcus Aelius Varro. He has come all the way from Rome, looking for a wife for one of the sons of Germanicus."

"Germanicus?" asked Herodias. "Wasn't he executed?"

"Executed? Of course not. Well, he fell ill and died," Marcus said. "Bad karma, it's said, due to his slanders of Emperor Tiberius."

"Is the eldest son, Nero, seeking a wife, or one of the younger brothers?  That is, is Salome to be a queen or merely a princess?"

"Ah," said Marcus. "You know the fickleness of fate. Sometimes things are very much like dominos.  Germanicus's eldest, Nero, has been accused of treason and conspiracy. And Drusus as well. So it would seem that the only son of Germanicus that is not implicit in such scheming is young Caligula."

"Caligula, then?" asked Herodias.  "He's seeking a wife?"

"Yes, the boy is fourteen.  He's heard of the beauty and ruthlessness of your daughter, Salome, and thinks he might find a kindred spirit in her.  If Caligula comes of age before Tiberius passes, she might become Empress of Rome."

"Ruthlessness? Sir, my daughter is nothing if not kind."

"Did she not ask for the head of John the Baptist on a platter?" asked Marcus, "even when offered half her father's kingdom?  It would seem to me she puts the value of carnage even higher than the value of power or money!"

"Yes, I suppose so.  No, the rumors are not entirely apocryphal.  But there may have been some nuance to the request, you don't fully appreciate."

"Of course.  I would not presume to make judgment.  But I think it would also be remiss not to share what captured young Caligula's...  heart?...  well...  attention.  His delight in the idea of her.  Storytellers have embellished the tale for him.  He imagines Salome thrusting her hand into John's chest and pulling out his heart. He imagines her dancing and talking with his decapitated head like a sock puppet."

Herodias grimaced. "Yes. I'm sure you have very good story-tellers. In truth, the body was sent directly to John's disciples."

A slight smile came to Salome.  "After all, I am loathe to touch the blood of a pig."

Marcus regarded Salome with some curiosity. Her eyes stared into the distance, conveying something he could not quite read. "Rumor in Rome is that John was highly respected," Marcus said.  "I've been very curious as to how you determined he was a pig?"

Salome's smile widens.  "Ah, you think I'm being figurative.  You like my riddle?" 

"What...  riddle?  You're saying John is a literal pig?" asked Marcus.  

Salome started to say more, but Herodias put her arm on Marcus' shoulder, and laughed, "Never mind.  She can go on sometimes...  What were you saying about young Caligula?"

"Well, so long as the storytellers keep Caligula's interest on the macabre, rather than on the rule of Rome, he remains in Sejanus's good graces.  They've developed somewhat of a rapport."

Herodias said, "Oh, so this Sejanus...  He's got the ear of the Emperor Tiberius?  What about the handsome Marcus Aelius Varro?  Does he have any pull?"

" Kyria, Mistress, I am merely the councilman who was chosen to determine Salome's suitability, and to sing the praises of Caligula, so that the marriage might be arranged."

"Well, Caligula sounds completely horrid," pouted Salome.

"Ah, my little Trojan horse," said Herodias. "Let's not chase the man away just yet. Just think of the possibility of being Empress...  and the luxury of Rome."

For a moment, Marcus wondered... "Trojan horse?   Riddles, riddles."  Then it struck him. "Ah yes, Mistress.  Yes, with a son in your womb, Rome itself may suit you."

Salome frowned, eyes narrowing, as she put both hands on her belly.

"Well," said Herod, "Roman Law permits marriage, at twelve, but I believe the legal age is fourteen, before you can start thinking of loading up my daughter with Trojans."

"Good sir," winked Herodias.  "Perhaps, while you're in the area, you would like to visit the site where Jesus of Nazareth came to honor the passing of his friend John on the shore of Galilee."

Herod said, "The shore of Tiberias."

Herodias said, "Yes, now we've renamed it after the emperor Tiberius."

Herod said, "Misspelled, of course."

"Wait...  I've heard about this! Bethsaida, right?  It's already somewhat of a tourist attraction in the region. The one where the bread multiplied to feed five thousand?  It's a popular story.  But in some circles, they're saying the Gentiles just ate the forbidden bread thrown out by the Jews, instead of starving themselves."

Salome said, "It was weeks ago.  Passover is still coming up."

"Well, then," said Marcus, "I guess we'll see the place near Passover.  Anyway, I'm not above having a little forbidden bread."

Herodias continued, "I'm sure my husband Herod is too busy, but you can accompany Salome and me."

Herod said, "Going away with yet another man, Herodias? Let's not forget this time...  Sundays are for worship.  Be back in time to kneel."

Herodias said, "Yes, my love. As always.  I will fast and worship at your private shrine, of course."

### Salome and the Draupadi Precedent

That evening, Salome found her mother in the women's quarters, brushing her hair before bed.

"Mommy," Salome said quietly, "I don't want to marry Caligula."

Herodias set down her brush. "I know, my little Trojan horse. But Marcus's plan is sound. If you arrive in Rome already pregnant, you can secure queenship until your child comes of age.  Caligula would have no claim to fatherhood."

Salome frowned. "But who would father my child? Marcus?"

Herodias smiled. "That is your choice, my dear. You are twelve now. Old enough to choose."

Salome's eyes lit up. "Didn't Draupi have five husbands? Why can't I?"

Herodias raised an eyebrow. "You remember the story of Draupi and the five Pandavas?"

"Of course," Salome said. "When Arjuna won her hand at the swayamvara, his mother Kunti told him to share his prize with his brothers. So Draupi married all five of them."

Herodias studied her daughter for a long moment. "And who would you choose, if you could have five husbands?"

Salome counted on her fingers. "Barabbas, because he and I have become great friends through the riddle games. John, because I got to know him when he was imprisoned, and he's kind. Marcus, because he disgusts Caligula and I trust him. And..."

"And?" Herodias prompted.

"I want to meet more people first," Salome said. "Maybe one of the disciples Jesus of Nazareth left behind in Capernaum."

Herodias laughed. "Very well. We shall visit Capernaum. And if any man wishes to marry you, he must follow the Jewish customs and be circumcised."

Salome nodded solemnly. "Just like Jacob told Shechem."

Herodias's smile faded. "Yes. But let us hope your suitors are more honorable than Simeon and Levi were to Shechem's men."

---

### Philip the Tetrarch Arrives

The next morning, Philip the Tetrarch arrived at Herod's palace.  He was a man of forty-six years, with graying hair and a dignified bearing.  He had heard of Salome's beauty and had come to Tiberias to see her for himself.

"Herodias," Philip said, bowing slightly. "I have heard rumors of your daughter's...  situation. I wish to be considered as a suitor."

Herodias regarded him coolly. "Philip.  You are my late husband's half-brother. Do you truly wish to marry your niece?"

Philip smiled. "The Herodian family has never been squeamish about such matters. Besides, I can offer Salome stability, wealth, and a kingdom of her own."

Salome stepped forward. "Uncle Philip, I have heard you are a lover of horses."

Philip nodded. "I am. I keep the finest stables in all of Judea."

"Then you must know," Salome said, "that a mare will not accept a stallion who has not been properly prepared for her. I have become Jewish.  Any man who wishes to marry me must be circumcised."

Philip's face paled slightly, but he held his composure. "I see. And where would I find a mohel skilled enough for...  adult circumcision?"

Herodias said, "There is a man in Capernaum.  Judas Iscariot.  He is the grandson of Simeon, who circumcised Jesus of Nazareth when he was an infant.  He practices the ancient rite for adult Gentiles who convert to Judaism."

Philip nodded slowly. "Very well. I will seek him out."

---

### Marcus's Proposal

That afternoon, Marcus found Salome walking in the gardens. She was humming a tune he did not recognize.

"Lady Salome," Marcus said, bowing.  "May I speak with you?"

Salome stopped and turned to face him. "Of course, Marcus. What is it?"

Marcus hesitated. "I... I cannot bring myself to sing Caligula's praises any longer. The boy is a monster. I fear what Rome will become under his rule."

Salome studied him for a long moment. "Then why not serve yourself instead of serving him?"

Marcus blinked. "What do you mean?"

"Marry me," Salome said simply.  "Father my child. You would be the grandfather of a future emperor. And I would be safe from Caligula's whims."

Marcus stared at her, speechless. 

Salome smiled. "But you must be circumcised. That is the condition."

Marcus let out a long breath. "I...  I will do it.  For you, and for Rome."

---

### Judas Iscariot Introduced

Judas Iscariot was a man of thirty years, with sharp eyes and steady hands. He had been trained in the ancient rite of *Metzitzeh b'peh*—the oral suctioning of blood after circumcision—and was known throughout Galilee as the most skilled mohel for adult Gentiles who converted to Judaism. 

When Philip and Marcus came to him, Judas greeted them with a mixture of curiosity and caution.  "You wish to be circumcised?  For what purpose?"

Philip said, "To marry Salome, daughter of Herodias."

Marcus said, "To secure my future—and hers."

Judas studied them both, then nodded. "Very well. I will perform the rite. But know this: the pain will be great, and the recovery long. You must be prepared."

Philip said, "I am prepared."

Marcus said, "As am I."

---

### The Circumcision

The circumcision took place in a private room near the synagogue. Judas laid out his instruments—a sharp knife, a bowl of water, and strips of cloth for binding. 

He began with Philip.  The tetrarch gritted his teeth as Judas made the cut, and then performed the *Metzitzeh b'peh*, suctioning the blood with his mouth. Philip's face turned pale, but he did not cry out.

Next came Marcus. The Roman envoy closed his eyes and tried to steady his breathing. When Judas finished, Marcus let out a low groan, but he too remained silent.

Judas washed his hands and said, "It is done. You will need several days to heal.  Do not exert yourselves."

---

### Salome's "Earrings"

When Philip and Marcus emerged from the room, pale and unsteady, Salome was waiting for them. She held out her hand. 

"Give them to me," she said. 

Philip blinked. "Give you...  what?"

"Your foreskins," Salome said calmly. "I want them."

Marcus's eyes widened. "You... you want our foreskins?"

"Yes," Salome said.  "I will wear them as earrings. So that everyone will know I have the ear of the king, and the ear of the horse-lover."

Judas, who had been cleaning his instruments, paused and looked at Salome with a mixture of horror and fascination. He handed her two small pieces of flesh, wrapped in cloth. 

Salome took them and held them up to the light. "Look, Mother," she called.  "This one is Malchus's Ear. And this one is Philip's Ear."

Herodias, who had been watching from the doorway, covered her mouth with her hand. "Salome..."

Salome smiled. "It's perfect. Now everyone will know they have my ear."

### Salome Meets Andrew

A week later, Herodias and Salome traveled to Capernaum, accompanied by Marcus.  They came to see the place where Jesus of Nazareth had fed five thousand. 

As they walked along the shore, Salome noticed a man mending nets by the water. His hands moved with steady rhythm, weaving the torn mesh back together. He looked up, squinting in the sun, and smiled — not the calculating smile of Marcus, nor the cautious deference of Philip, but something simpler. A fisherman's smile.

"Mommy," Salome said quietly, pointing. "If you got Gabriel, can I have that guy?"

Herodias followed her gaze and thought back.  "I think he used to travel with Jesus of Nazareth."

They watched and some other men approached him, and Herodias said, "And those...  I think they might be Barabbas' older brothers.  They used to travel with John."

The men worked together for a little while, talking, explaining, listening, nodding, separating and untangling things.  

Salome watched him return to his nets. "He looks... kind.  He could be a suitor.  How should I test him?"

Herodias laughed, "I'm not quite sure that's how suitors work.  Generally they're supposed to come to you."

Salome shrugs.  "He already has come to me.  Did you see that smile?"

Marcus raised an eyebrow. "A fisherman? For the future bride of Caligula Caesar?"

Salome didn't look away from Andrew. "He only needs a little more encouragement."

Herodias studied her daughter for a long moment. "We shall see, my little Trojan horse. Let us meet with the prophets first."

"If you're trying to impress a kind man, You might want to take off those earrings, before he asks about them." said Marcus.

"But if he passes my test, two little things like these won't bother him." said Salome.

### The Meeting with Jesus Barabbas and John

They approached the five east-asian men who were helping with the nets.  "Are you Draupi's sons?  We're looking for Barabbas."  They pointed toward the north.  "Yes, up that stream, they're digging for clay."  

They found Jesus Barabbas and John hard at work, hands caked with mud, filling a wheel-barrow with clay. The earth smelled rich and wet, and the rhythmic scrape of their shovels echoed against the streambed. When they got within sight, Salome sprinted forward. 

"What are you doing?" asked Salome.  

Barabbas stood up and rubbed his back.  "We're preparing to build a small scale replica of Jerusalem." he said.

Salome said, "Whoah...  Where are you building it?"

Barabbas says, "We have a studio here in Capernaum.  But Lord knows how we'll pay the second month's rent, haha."  

When Herodias approached, John stood and bowed, "Herodias, good to see you."

Herodias said, "John, I'm so glad you're better."

John said, "Yes, the drug you and Gabriel used on me nearly ended me.  But apparently the ruse paid off."

Barabbas and Salome were already playing word-games.  "and, I told him I would never touch the blood of a pig."  

"Ah.  Because it was a pig's blood," Barabbas said, "Very clever."

"See, somebody gets it." said Salome.

Herodias sighs.  "Her riddles are going to give away our ruse.  She was going on like that right in front of Herod!"

"Oh my, dear, you're getting your dress dirty.  That's a more sensual hug than an old man like me deserves from a young lady."

Herodias laughs, "Salome is sowing her wild oats, Barabbas.  Since she reached marrying age, she already has two suitors.  And she's seeking more."

"Not just suitors, Barabbas," Salome said, still clinging to him despite the mud.  "Husbands.  I want to do like your mom.  Five seems just right."

"Interesting," said Barabbas, "and what are your expectations for these five husbands?  Will they be kept men?  Or are you of the opinion if you love somebody, set them free?"

Salome pulled back, finger aside her face, thinking.  "Hmmmm." she said, "I will put you in a cage with the model of Jerusalem you're building.  And Marcus... I don't know what I'll do with him.  John...  I've already had him in a cage...  I guess I prefer to set him free."  

"Well, as it happens, I'm to spend many days with this model of Jerusalem anyway.  So long as I can go free once I'm done with it, I see no harm in being a kept man." Barabbas says.

John says, "Can I just be your secret husband?  I'd rather not call attention that I'm still alive."

"Of course, John.  Really, you always have been." she winks.

Marcus cleared his throat. "I am Marcus Aelius Varro, envoy of Caligula Caesar. I have come to determine Salome's suitability as a bride for the son of Germanicus."

Barabbas's eyebrows rose. "Caligula?  The boy who delights in stories of blood and mayhem?"

Marcus grimaced. "The very same. Though I confess, I find his tastes... distasteful."

Herodias said, "Marcus has been honest with us. He does not love Caligula.  But he serves Rome, and Rome requires this match."

Salome spoke up.  "I have no desire to marry Caligula. But I am willing to go to Rome—if I can secure my own future, and not be subject to his whims."

Marcus nodded. "A wise strategy. If you were already with child when you arrived in Rome, you could secure queenship until the child comes of age.  Caligula would have no claim to fatherhood, and you would rule as regent."

Herodias smiled. "An excellent plan. But any man who wishes to father Salome's child must meet one condition."

"What condition?" Marcus asked. 

Salome stepped forward, her voice clear and firm. "I have become Jewish. Any man who wishes to marry me—or even to be considered—must be circumcised."

Barabbas and John exchanged a glance. 

John said quietly, "I was circumcised as a baby."

Barabbas said, "As you know, I was raised as a girl. I have not yet borne that mark.  But if I am to be counted worthy—as a prophet to the people—I must still do so."

Salome's eyes lit up. "Then you will seek out Judas Iscariot?"

Barabbas said, "I am... currently holding off on the circumcision detail.  After all the wedding details, Mistress Salome, you still have a couple years of freedom from child-bearing.  We cannot make a king by force."

Salome said "Should I take you, then, as wife, rather than husband?"

Barabbas nodded. "I think it is right."

"Great!" said Salome.  "That's four, I just need one more.  Who is that hunk down at the beach working with your brothers?"

### The Proposal

Andrew, who had been standing silently by the shore, cleared his throat. "I... I don't understand what's happening. But if you're asking me to marry Lady Salome, I should tell you: I have no wealth. Only my nets and my loyalty."

Salome stepped forward and took his hand. "That's all I need. The others bring money and power. You bring honesty."

Andrew's face turned red, but he didn't pull away. 

"But you're planning to marry five men?   All at once?"

"It's nothing official," Herodias said.   "Legally, it's not even a thing.   And of course, you have two years to wait before any sort of consummation can even be considered.  But she's of age to be married, and she's got it in her head to be like Draupadi."

Barabbas raised a hand, "Well, there is a technicality.   Draupadi spent a year with each of her husbands, one-by-one.   No single night of quintuple matrimony.  And the whole thing was started by confusion when Kunti told her to share the winnings of the contest between the five brothers."

Salome said, "Yes!   A contest, then.  I love contests."  

Herodias clapped her hands. "The contest will have many parts. Each suitor will demonstrate his worthiness in the ways that matter most to my daughter."

Salome's eyes lit up. "Yes! A bread competition — who can provide the most?  A wine competition — who can pour the best vintage? A riddle competition — who can make me laugh?  A cuddle competition — who can make me feel safest?  And a handbasket competition — who can fill the most?"

Barabbas raised an eyebrow. "A handbasket competition? What does that entail?"

Salome shrugged. "We'll figure it out on the mountain. But I want to see who can really *provide*. Not just promise."

John murmured, "And what of those who have nothing material to offer?"

Salome stepped forward and took his hand. "Then they offer what they have.  Stars, songs, loyalty, love."

Herodias nodded.  "The contest will take place in three days' time on the mountain where Jesus of Nazareth fed the five thousand.  Prepare yourselves."

As the group dispersed, John lingered near Barabbas. "Five husbands," he muttered. "I thought I was the only one who loved you."

Barabbas smiled. "Love is not diminished by sharing. It's multiplied."

John frowned. "But that's if you can put away jealousy.  Draupadi's husbands never had to see each other with her."

"Then Salome presents us with a more significant challenge," Barabbas said. And he left John alone in his thoughts, not realizing where John's jealousy lay.
