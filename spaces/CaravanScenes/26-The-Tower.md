Tarot-Arcana:  My concept of "The Tower" Tarot card is to look at what happens when the whole thing burns down--when your system is crumbled by an earthquake, and what foundations you have left.  The different witnesses to Jesus' return reveal the expectations of the gospel-writers' voices.
Verses: 
 - "*Mat 28:16-20* — Great Commission — Jesus appears to the Eleven on a mountain in Galilee"
 - "*Mark 16:1-8*: Women say NOTHING, *Mark 16:9-20* (Disputed) Jesus appears  to Mary Magdalene, then two walking in the country, then the Eleven at table"
 - *Luke 24:13-35* — Road to Emmaus — Jesus appears to Clopas and Simon, recognized in breaking of bread
 - *Luke 24:36-49* — Jesus appears to the Eleven in Jerusalem, eats fish, commissions them
 - *Luke 24:50-53* — Ascension at Bethany, disciples return to Jerusalem praising God

# Draft

The Passover of 30 CE was a special one because there were two days of Sabbath in a row.

After the three days the women were coming to the tomb where they saw Joseph of Arimathea bring Jesus of Nazareth.  They had not seen Nicodemus with him when they buried Jesus Barabbas in the garden with 75 pounds of spices and herbs.  Therefore Joanna and Salome brought spices, and expected that the tomb should already stink.

By the final report, The women had found the stone rolled away from the tomb, and when they entered, they did not find the body.  (**10** It was Mary Magdalene, Salome, wife of Philip, and her servant Joanna, wife of Chuza, Mary, wife of Clopas, Mary the mother of James, and the others with them who told this to the apostles.)

While they were wondering about this, suddenly two men in clothes that gleamed like lightning stood beside them.  

This report somewhat matched Mary Magdalene's original report that two angels sat at the head and foot where Jesus body had been, however, Salome and Joanna, being familiar with more sophisticated attire, registered the two as men in gleaming clothes rather than angels.

Indeed, it only occurred to Salome and Joanna to bow down to the ground after the other ladies had already done so in fright.   And even then, it was mostly to humor them.

But the men said to them, “Why do you look for the living among the dead? **6** He is not here; he has risen! Remember how he told you, while he was still with you in Galilee: **7** ‘The Son of Man must be delivered over to the hands of sinners, be crucified and on the third day be raised again.’”

**8** Then they remembered his words.  

And Salome whispered, "Yes, The Son of Man had to be delivered to the sinners!  But not the Messiah!  Jesus of Nazareth must still be alive!"  

And Joanna whispered back, "But if he's alive, is it Barabbas, then, who comes back on the third day?"

**9** When they came back from the tomb, they told all these things to the Eleven and to all the others.  **11** Though Clopas believed his wife Mary, and John and Thomas remained quiet, none of the other disciples believed the women, because their words seemed to them like nonsense. 

Now John, in his later writing would claim that he went immediately with Peter, before dawn, and before any other woman except Mary Magdalene.  He did this to fulfill his role as Maher Shalal Hash Baz.  

But before John understood that he had to make Jesus Barabbas return from the dead, the Lukes would report that of the disciples, it was only Peter that ran to the tomb, and only after he had heard from seven or more women.

Peter had helped bury the corpse in the Garden, and had not known anything about the tomb until this moment.  

**12** So Peter got up and ran to the tomb. Bending over, he saw the strips of linen lying by themselves, and he went away, wondering to himself what had happened, but electing not to tell anyone that the body had been buried in the Garden.

### On the Road to Emmaus

**13** Meanwhile Thomas joined Clopas and Mary headed northwest toward Galilee.  They were going to stop in a village straight west,  about seven miles from Jerusalem, called Emmaus on their way. **14** They were talking with each other about everything that had happened on their way out of Jerusalem. **15** As they talked and discussed these things with each other, a man they did not recognize came up to them.

**17** He asked them, “What are you discussing together as you walk along?” 

They stood still, their faces downcast. **18** Mary asked him, “Are you the only one visiting Jerusalem who does not know the things that have happened there in these days?”  

**19** “What things?” he asked.  

“About Jesus of Nazareth,” they replied. “He was a prophet, powerful in word and deed before God and all the people. **20** The chief priests and our rulers handed him over to be sentenced to death, and they crucified him; 

**21** Thomas added, "we had hoped that he was the one who was going to redeem Israel."

Mary added, "And what is more, it is the third day since all this took place."

Clopas said, "**22** In addition, some of our women amazed us. They went to the tomb early this morning **23** but didn’t find his body. They came and told us that they had seen a vision of angels, who said he was alive.


Thomas said "**24** Then some of our companions went to the tomb and found it just as the women had said, but they did not see Jesus.”

The man **25** said to them, “How foolish you are, and how slow to believe all that the prophets have spoken! **26** Did the Messiah have to suffer these things and then enter his glory?” 

"I suppose so." said Thomas.

"No!" he said.  

**27** And beginning with Moses and all the Prophets, he explained to them what was said in all the Scriptures concerning himself.

He explained how Barabbas, John, Jesus of Nazareth, and Judas shared the duties of prophecy.  How Jesus Barabbas and Jesus of Nazareth served as the Lord's goat and scapegoat.  How John would now confess the sins of Israel over the head of the scapegoat, how John would write Jesus Barabbas as the Thief in the Night, and the Queen of the South.  How he had performed the resurrection of Lazarus as the Sign of Jonah for the wicked generation.  How Jesus Barabbas, taking it upon himself to warn of no sins was being held accountable by God for every sin you can die from.  How Jesus Barabbas and John had been anointed on the feet as two lampstands one either side of the Lord.  How Jesus of Nazareth had been anointed on the head as Lord.  How Peter would take up the equipment of the foolish shepherd.  

They wondered, "If none of these prophecies were fulfilled by Jesus of Nazareth, then what did he fulfill?"

Jesus of Nazareth will not break a reed or snuff out a candle, he said.  He fulfilled the prophecy of Immanuel bringing the principles of God with us.  He also set up the aberration that causes desolation when he said you should not forgive seven times but seventy times seven times.

Thomas said, "How does setting up endless forgiveness put the aberration that causes...  Oh...  Wait, I get it."

"And Judas," Jesus said, by committing suicide, broke the staff of union, and the staff of favor between Israel and Judah.  But this was required by prophecy.  

"One could fulfill one more prophecy," he said, looking at Thomas.  "Say, with a well placed candle and lens...  in a locked room.  You two know that Jesus of Nazareth has gone to Galilee.  But the disciples are not yet ready to hear what he has to say there, because John has yet to write the Riddles of the Father and Son."

"What could one do with such a well placed candle and lens?"

"Has Jesus not said to you, 'they will say, here he is in a locked room, but do not believe them'?"

Thomas nodded.  "Yes, he taught that shortly before he died."

Then appear to them in a locked room.  Tell them Jesus of Nazareth is not ready for them to come to Galilee yet.  They must immerse themselves in the Holy Spirit long before they are ready for the Riddles of the Father and The Son.  Only once they have the Holy Spirit can they baptize in the Father and the Son.  

**28** As they approached the village to which they were going, Jesus continued on as if he were going farther. **29** But they urged him strongly, “Stay with us, for it is nearly evening; the day is almost over.” So he went in to stay with them.

**30** The next morning, when he was at the table with them, he took bread, gave thanks, broke it and his hood fell back, revealing his face, as he began to give it to them. **31** Then their eyes were opened and they recognized him, and he put the hood back up and disappeared from their sight. **32** They asked each other, “Were not our hearts burning within us while he talked with us on the road and opened the Scriptures to us?”

**33** They got up and returned at once to Jerusalem.  Thomas went to set up his trick, but Clopas found the rest of the Eleven and those with them, assembled them together in a dark room, where Thomas had hidden, **34** and saying, “It is true! The Lord has risen and has appeared to Simon.”  Simon Peter recalled burying the body with Andrew but he changed a couple of details.  He claimed to have put the body in the tomb, instead of burying it in the garden, and he claimed repeated Andrew's words "Jesus called you Cephas because if you don't praise him, the rocks will cry out," as though Jesus himself had said them.

**35** Then Mary and Clopas told what had happened on the way, and how Jesus was recognized by them when he broke the bread.

Jesus Appears to the Disciples
**36** While they were still talking about this, Thomas lit a candle, and it seemed the image of Jesus himself stood among them.  He said to them, “Peace be with you.”  

It was actually Thomas, who had covered his face with make-up and blood, so his face looked scarred and beaten.  He spoke with a lisp as though his lips were busted.  His arms and feet showed behind the counter, as his image flickered against the wall by the candle-light.

**37** They were startled and frightened, thinking they saw a ghost. **38** Thomas slurred to them, “Why are you troubled, and why do doubts rise in your minds? **39** Look at my hands and my feet. It is I myself! Touch me and see; a ghost does not have flesh and bones, as you see I have.”

Though he never said "I am Jesus of Nazareth" many believed he was.

**40** When he had said this, he showed them his hands and feet. **41** And while they still did not believe it because of joy and amazement, he asked them, “Do you have anything here to eat?” **42** They gave him a piece of broiled fish, into his physical hand, a hand bleeding from the nail hole.  **43** and he took the hand and passed it to his face, which they could see in the flickering image on the the wall.

**44** He said to them, “This is what I told you while I was still with you: Everything must be fulfilled that is written about me in the Law of Moses, the Prophets and the Psalms.”

**45** Then he opened their minds so they could understand the Scriptures. **46** He told them, “This is what is written: The Messiah will suffer and rise from the dead on the third day, **47** and repentance for the forgiveness of sins will be preached in his name to all nations, beginning at Jerusalem. **48** You are witnesses of these things. **49** I am going to send you what my Father has promised; but stay in the city until you have been clothed with power from on high.”  *Acts 1:4-8* "Do not leave Jerusalem, but wait for the gift my Father promised, which you heard me speak about.  For John baptized with water, but in a few days you will be baptized with the Holy Spirit."

He said to them: “It is not for you to know the times or dates the Father has set by his own authority. But you will receive power when the Holy Spirit comes on you; and you will be my witnesses in Jerusalem, and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the ends of the earth.”

After that, Thomas pulled his cloak more tightly about him, and said no more.  He came out from behind the counter and walked, silently

**50** When he had led them out to the vicinity of Bethany, he lifted up his hands and blessed them. **51** He pointed away, toward the stars, and then another man, whose voice sounded more like Jesus, said a long prayer, and their eyes were gazing up at the clouds, Thomas and the other man quickly doffed their cloaks and flipped them inside out.

*Acts 1:10* They were intently looking up into the sky, and when they looked back, Thomas and the other man were dressed in white.

When they opened their eyes, Cleopas said, "he left us and was taken up into heaven." 

*Acts 1:11* "Men of Galilee," the other man said, "why do you stand here looking into the sky?  This same Jesus, who has been taken from you into heaven, will come back in the same way you have seen him go into heaven."

**52** Then they worshiped him and returned to Jerusalem with great joy.

**1:12** Then the apostles returned to Jerusalem from the hill called the Mount of Olives, about half a mile from the city. 
**1:13** When they arrived, they went upstairs to the room where they were staying. Those present were Peter, John, James and Andrew; Philip and Thomas, Bartholomew and Matthew; James son of Alphaeus and Simon the Zealot, and Judas son of James. 

**53** And they stayed continually at the temple, praising God.  **1:14** They all joined together constantly in prayer, along with the women and Mary the mother of Jesus, and with his brothers.
 
*Acts 1:15-22* Peter preached upon how Judas Iscariot had filled prophecy, not by the kiss, but by his suicide, and how another must take his place.

*Acts 1:23-26*  They nominated two men; Barsabbas, who had traveled with Barabbas, and Matthias, who had traveled with Jesus of Nazareth.  They cast lots, and the lot fell to Matthias.

By this time the movement had more than 120 members, and many among them were literate and bilingual.  Therefore, they chose some of the teachings of Jesus of Nazareth and began writing them down, and translating them into many languages.

*Acts 2:1* When the day of Pentecost came, they were all together in one place, practicing the reading of scrolls in many different languages.  **2** The sound was like the blowing of a violent wind coming from heaven and filling the whole house where they were sitting.  **3** The scrolls were like tongues of fire that came to rest on each of them.  **4** All of them were filled with the Holy Spirit and began to speak in other tongues as the Spirit enabled them.

**5** Now there were staying in Jerusalem God-fearing Jews from every nation under heaven. **6** When they heard this sound, a crowd came together in bewilderment, because each one heard their own language being spoken. **7** Utterly amazed, they asked: “Aren’t all these who are speaking Galileans? **8** Then how is it that each of us hears them in our native language? **9** Parthians, Medes and Elamites; residents of Mesopotamia, Judea and Cappadocia, Pontus and Asia,[b] **10** Phrygia and Pamphylia, Egypt and the parts of Libya near Cyrene; visitors from Rome **11** (both Jews and converts to Judaism); Cretans and Arabs—we hear them declaring the wonders of God in our own tongues!” **12** Amazed and perplexed, they asked one another, “What does this mean?”

**13** Some, however, made fun of them and said, “They have had too much wine.”

Peter began preaching against the Jews, calling those who had fulfilled prophecy wicked men.  Accusing them of nailing the Messiah to the cross, though it had actually been Jesus Barabbas.  He spoke of another Lord saying to David's Lord, "Sit at my right hand until I make your enemies a footstool for your feet."

And he claimed that the Messiah was both the Lord and the Lord of David, so the people asked, "Brothers, what shall we do?"

Rightly, Peter replied, "Repent and be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ to receive the gift of the Holy Spirit," which opened some gates to heaven, and closed some others.

About three thousand were baptized that day.

*Acts 3* One day, Peter and John found a man lame from birth, and Peter, through his words, happened to be able to heal a man of his afflictions.  People were amazed at what had happened him.  But Peter's preaching did not praise the man's faith.  Rather Peter focused on the name of Jesus as the source of power.  Moreover, he quickly turned his preaching to the shame of the Jews, rather the celebration of those with faith.

This angered the priests and temple guards so Peter and John were arrested and imprisoned for one night.

In the morning, they asked "By what power or what name did you do this," to which Jesus of Nazareth always had answered "it is your faith that healed you." 

But Peter said, "It is by the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth"  

He said, "Salvation is found in no one else" for Peter had never trusted in the Parable of the sheep and the goats.  He was determined to use Jesus name like a magical power-word.

*Acts 4:18-20* But the pharisees and temple guard were also confused, for they sought to stop people from saying Jesus.  Of course this only made the practice of calling on Jesus name more attractive to all who resisted authority.

*Acts 4:32-37* Wealthy people were even coming to the Christians and selling all their possessions to provide for the commune.

*Acts 5:1-10* And soon Peter became drunk with the power of Jesus' name, that he began using it for evil.  When Ananias and Sepphira came to him saying that they had sold everything they owned for the commune, Peter sensed they were holding back and accused them.  He called this "conspiring to test the Spirit of the Lord".  

Because Paul had not yet clarified "Faith is evidence of what you hope for". Peter had abused faith, and caused evidence of what Ananias and Sapphira dreaded.  Thus they fell, dead to Peter.

Great fear seized the whole church.  Their faith had been replaced with fear.  And now they put their hopes and fears into Peter.  The sick hoped that Peter's shadow would fall across them.  Hearing that Peter could kill by shame impressed the crowd even more than hearing that he could heal via faith.  

Though now people were being healed by their faith in Peter's name, he still did not realize that it was faith that healed people, rather than any specific name.

*Acts 5:17-24* But the high priest of Pharisees, and the Saducees were jealous of Peter, and arrested the apostles.  Someone in the guise of an angel let them out, got them all past the guards, and the captain of the temple guard was at a loss

*Acts 5:28-33* The apostles were frustrated that Peter was accusing them of killing Jesus of Nazareth.  And his defense, accusing them of being against God and the Holy Spirit, made them furious.

*Acts 5:34-40* Gamaliel, a teacher of the law, convinced them to let them go, by a call to the same Holy Spirit by which Peter accused them.

*Acts 5:41* But the apostles interpreted the mercy as disgrace.  *5:42* And they never stopped conflating the Messiah and the Son of Man.

*Acts 9* Now, there was a man named Saul who had heard of how Peter had used the fear of Jesus to kill Ananias and Sepphira.  This made him breathe out murderous threats agaisnt the Lord's disciples.  He was going to an authority in Damascus to ask for licenses to arrest anyone who belonged to this sect, but while he was on his way, he met Jesus of Nazareth, who was not dead.  Then shortly afterward, he met Ananias, who also was not dead.

Jesus of Nazareth told Ananias, and Ananias told Paul "Brother Saul, the Lord - Jesus, who appeared to you on the road as you were coming here - has sent me so that you may see again and be filled with the Holy Spirit".  

Saul spent several days with Jesus, Ananias, and the other disciples in Damascus.  

He heard much of the wisdom of Jesus of Nazareth.  Jesus explained to him that Peter had mis-used his name in dealing with Ananias, not invoking faith, but something else.  For, he told him, "Faith is evidence of what is hoped for."  He told him many other things, and Paul got much right, even proving that Jesus of Nazareth was the Messiah.  Barnabus saw him preaching in Damascus and reported this to the more public group of disciples in Jerusalem.
