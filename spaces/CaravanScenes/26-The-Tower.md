# 26-The-Tower

### Sabbaths and the Empty Tomb

The Passover of 30 CE was a special one because there were two days of Sabbath in a row.

After the three days the women were coming to the tomb where they saw Joseph of Arimathea bring Jesus of Nazareth.  They had not seen Nicodemus with him when they buried Jesus Barabbas in the garden with 75 pounds of spices and herbs.  Therefore Joanna and Salome brought spices, and expected that the tomb should already stink.

### The Witness of the Women

By the final report, The women had found the stone rolled away from the tomb, and when they entered, they did not find the body.  (**10** It was Mary Magdalene, Salome, wife of Philip, and her servant Joanna, wife of Chuza, Mary, wife of Clopas, Mary the mother of James, and the others with them who told this to the apostles.)

While they were wondering about this, suddenly two men in clothes that gleamed like lightning stood beside them.  

This report somewhat matched Mary Magdalene's original report that two angels sat at the head and foot where Jesus body had been, however, Salome and Joanna, being familiar with more sophisticated attire, registered the two as men in gleaming clothes rather than angels.

Indeed, it only occurred to Salome and Joanna to bow down to the ground after the other ladies had already done so in fright.   And even then, it was mostly to humor them.

**8** Then they remembered his words.

And Joanna whispered back, "But if he's alive, is it Barabbas, then, who comes back on the third day?"

**9** They went back to the Eleven to say what they had seen and heard. **11** Though Clopas believed his wife Mary, John and Thomas remained quiet, while the other nine simply didn't believe them.

Peter had helped Andrew bury the corpse in the garden, and had not known anything about the tomb until this moment.

John said, "Jesus already raised Lazarus from the dead.  That was the sign for the wicked and adulterous generation.  Why, therefore, should Jesus also be raised from the dead?"

### Peter Runs to the Tomb 

**12** Meanwhile Peter, by himself, got up and ran to the tomb.  Inside the tomb, he knelt, seeing  the strips of linen lying by themselves.  This was not where he had buried the body, and he went away, wondering who had arranged this trick.  But until he found out, he would tell no one of the body he and Andrew buried in the garden.

### On the Road to Emmaus

**13** Clopas, the one who believed his wife, Mary, immediately set out toward Galilee, on the road toward Emmaus.  Before they got far, they found Thomas, who was already on his way as well.  

**14** Thomas, Clopas, and Mary were talking about Jesus' trial, Pilate's choice, Jesus' crucifixion, and the disappearance of the body.  They all believed that Barabbas had fled without a word, Jesus of Nazareth had been crucified, and someone had hidden the body.  But the empty tomb gave them hope.

**15** And soon, they found yet another man, who was waiting at the edge of Jerusalem for a traveling companion.  It was Jesus of Nazareth, but they did not recognize him because he wore a false beard, and a cloak over his head, to keep himself unseen. 

**17** Some traveling companions were selective about what strangers they walked with, and this cloaked man seemed no different.  He asked them, "I'm looking for someone to walk with me to Emmaeus.  What have you been talking about along the way?"

They stopped, looking down.  **18** Mary asked him, “The same thing everyone is talking about!  Doesn't everyone in Jerusalem know what's been going on in these last few days?”  

**19** “Tell me.” he said.

“Jesus of Nazareth was crucified,” Clopas said.

"Go on," Jesus said.

Clopas replied. “We believe he was a prophet, He performed many miracles, and taught great things.  He was righteous before God and all the people. **20** Yet our chief priests and our rulers handed him over to Pilate to be sentenced to death, and the Romans crucified him." 

**21** Thomas added, "we had hoped that he was the one who was going to liberate Israel from the bonds of Rome."

Mary added, "But it is the third day since he was crucified.  Maybe Jesus will come back today to liberate Israel."

Clopas said, "**22** In addition, some of our women amazed us. They went to the tomb early this morning **23** but didn’t find his body. They came and told us that they had seen a vision of angels, who said he was alive.


Thomas said "**24** Then some of our companions went to the tomb and found it just as the women had said, but they did not see Jesus.”

The man **25** said to them, “You are all very earnest, but you lack a great deal of understanding, and you have not grasped how prophets words have been fulfilled!"

**26** Did the Messiah have to suffer these things and then enter his glory?” 

"I suppose so." said Thomas.

"Let us walk at a gentle pace" he said, "and allow your minds to join your hearts in trust.

**27** And beginning with Moses and all the Prophets, he explained to them what was said in all the Scriptures concerning himself.

This prophet, the Messiah, could never break the chains of Rome.  He could never even break a reed or snuff out a candle, he said.  

He could fulfill the prophecy of Immanuel bringing the principles of God with us.  God with our hearts, God with our minds.  God with our strength.    

Your "Jesus of Nazareth" he also set up the aberration that causes desolation, spoken of by the prophet Daniel.  

"What?  How?  Why would he do that?" asked Clopas.

"The correct question is how.  How could the Messiah set up the aberration that causes desolation in the middle of the seventieth seven while also never breaking a reed?"

Thomas finally said, "I have no idea.  It's impossible."

"Ah, but through the wonders of semantics, the Nazarene accomplished it.  When he was asked, 'how many times should I forgive my brother,' the Messiah answered, 'not seven times but seventy times seven times.'  How do you interpret this?"

Thomas said, "That we should always forgive, of course."

And Jesus said, "But what is the literal meaning?"

Cleopas, "That you may choose not to forgive after the seventieth seven of sins."

Jesus said, "Quite right, and what Jesus also taught was that should you not forgive your neighbor of their trespasses, God will also not forgive you. Nonforgiveness is the aberration that causes destruction."

"No." said Clopas and Mary.

But the god she asked could not put all these things in one person.  Instead, he provided her with five husbands.  Each one gave her one aspect of what she desired.  The same must be done for the prophecies of the Torah.  No single man can fulfill them all."

**28** When they came to Emmaeus, Jesus was going to continue on through the night toward Galilee, **29** But Thomas, Clopas, and Mary urged him strongly, “It's nearly evening.  Why don't you stop with us,” so he did.


###Recognized in the Breaking of Bread

**30** The next morning, at breakfast, Jesus took bread, gave thanks, and prayed.  "Dear Father in Heaven, let the disciples of Jesus of Nazareth be immersed in the Holy Spirit before they should finish any more prophecies.  May they do as little harm as possible.  May they be guided by righteosness, so that in their confusion of the Spirit of Truth they should only do good.  I pray for Thomas, and Clopas and Mary, and for all the disciples of Jesus of Nazareth that this message be received."

When he broke the bread, his hood revealed his face. **31** They recognized him before he pulled the hood back up around his face.  "I should go," he explained.  
**32** They asked each other, “Were not our hearts burning within us while he talked with us on the road and opened the Scriptures to us?”

Thomas said, "We must return to Jerusalem, to the others, and tell them to pray for the Holy Spirit before doing anything else."

But Mary and Clopas said, "they will not believe you."

Thomas nodded.  "Peter won't trust in evidence or reason.  But perhaps he may believe if I give him a miracle."

**33** They got up and returned at once to Jerusalem.  

### The Final Apparition

Clopas, Mary, and Thomas came back to Jerusalem in secret, then Thomas called upon his training with the Caravan of the Fifth wind to set up a darkened room near the home of the disciples.  He found one man among the actors; one who had the height and gait of Jesus of Nazareth, and he, himself, could do a great vocal impersonation of him.  He and the other man wore robes that appeared dark and hooded, but, when the proper string was pulled, the hood dropped down revealing a fabric of pure white.  

Now, he put make-up on the physical impersonator to bloody his face, his hands, and feet.  They set up a place behind a counter where he could show his hands and feet.  And they arranged a candle and lens so that the man's darkened and hooded face would be spookily projected onto the wall, like a ghost.

Finally, once everything was set up, Mary and Clopas found the eleven, and brought them back to the room.  

Those present were Peter, John, James and Andrew; Philip (and Thomas and the disguised man in secret), Bartholomew and Matthew; James son of Alphaeus and Simon the Zealot, and Judas son of James. 

Once they were all together, they noted, "Thomas couldn't join us.  He has gone to Galilee." and they were assembled together in the dark room **34** and saying, “It is true! The Lord has risen and has appeared to Simon.”

"Yes," Peter said,  "Jesus appeared to me last night, and said I would be called you Cephas because if I don't praise him, the rocks will cry out,"

He said this as though he had been at the tomb rather than in the garden, and as if Jesus himself had spoken to him instead of Andrew.

**35** Then Mary and Clopas told what had happened on their way to Emmaeus.  They didn't go into all the specifics, but said that the man told him how Jesus had fulfilled prophecy, and how they had recognized him by how he had broken bread.

**36** While they were still talking about this, Thomas lit a candle, and the actor appeared as an image of Jesus himself stood among them.  Thomas said, in his best Jesus-voice, “Peace be with you.”  

He spoke with a lisp as though his lips were busted.  The hands and feet showed behind the counter, as the cloaked image of his scarred and beaten face flickered against the wall by the candle-light.

**37** The disciples were startled and frightened, thinking the flickering image was surely a ghost. **38** Thomas slurred to them, “Why are you troubled, and why do doubts rise in your minds? **39** "Touch my hands and feet," he said."a ghost does not have flesh and bones, but, as you see I do.”

**40** Some reached out and touched his hands and feet.  Though he never technically said "I am Jesus of Nazareth" many believed he was.

**41** They still did not believe it.  Largely  because the trick was not fully convincing, yet they told themselves it was because they did not believe because they hoped too much, and the trick was too amazing.

Then Thomas said, “Do you have anything here to eat?” **42** So they put a piece of broiled fish, into his bleeding hand.  **43** The hand disappeared from the counter, and reappeared with the fish in the flickering image, where it entered his mouth.  

He chewed, swallowed, then said to them, **44** He said to them, “This is what I told you while I was still with you: Everything must be fulfilled that is written about me in the Law of Moses, the Prophets and the Psalms.”

"But pray for the Holy Spirit before fulfilling any more prophecies."

**45** Then, Thomas took up the tools of the foolish shepherd; he began to repeat Peter's false claims about scripture and what the prophet's required of the Messiah. **46** He told them, “This is what is written: The Messiah will suffer and rise from the dead on the third day.

Yet, he immersed things in the Holy Spirit by saying

"**47** Repentance for the forgiveness of sins will be preached in his name to all nations, beginning at Jerusalem. **48** You are witnesses of these things."  

But Peter was not a witness to repentance and forgiveness.  Instead, he heard the instruction to accuse the Jews, and preach against them in self-righteous anger.

But Thomas continued, **49** I am going to send you what my Father has promised; but stay in the city until you have been clothed with power from on high.”  John baptized with water, But the gift of my Father in Heaven is a baptism with the Holy Spirit.  Do not leave Jerusalem until you are immersed in the Holy Spirit.

But Thomas continued, “It is not for you to know the times or dates the Father has set by his own authority. But you will receive power when the Holy Spirit comes on you; and you will be my witnesses in Jerusalem, and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the ends of the earth.”

But Peter recalled Jesus saying to him "Whatever you bind on earth will be bound in heaven, and whatever you loose on earth will be loosed in heaven."  He believed this was the Holy Spirit, so he wielded Jesus name as a winnowing fork, taking the tools of the foolish shepherd.

### Ascension and the Two in White

After that, the body-double pulled his cloak more tightly about him.  He came out from behind the counter and walked, silently.  Thomas discretely joined them.

**50** And they walked from there, half a mile, all the way to Bethany, at the Mount of Olives, near Simon's house, where Jesus, John, and Barabbas had all been anointed; Jesus on the head, John and Barabbas on the feet. It was getting dark, and the stars were coming out.  Thomas stepped close to the other man, and blessed everyone in his best Jesus of Nazareth voice.  Then he looked up at the stars, until the others also looked. **51** Once their attention was elsewhere, Thomas and the other man pulled the strings in their robes, so that the hood fell away and revealed the bright white fabric within.  
*Acts 1:10* When the others looked back, Thomas and the other man were dressed in white.  Cleopas, who knew their trick, said, "he left us and was taken up into heaven." 

The other man, who had been Jesus' body-double said, *Acts 1:11* "Men of Galilee, why do you stand here looking into the sky?  Jesus, who has been taken from you into heaven, will come back in the same way you have seen him go into heaven."

**52** Then they turned back to the stars and worshiped.  Some worshiped God.  Some worshiped Jesus of Nazareth.  When finished, they returned Jerusalem with great joy.

### Apostolic Election and the Written Gospel

**53** They stayed for days, even weeks, at the temple, praising God.  **1:14** They all joined together constantly in prayer, along with the women and Mary the mother of Jesus, and with his brothers.
 
*Acts 1:15-22* Peter preached upon how Judas Iscariot had filled prophecy, not by the kiss, but by his suicide, and how another must take his place.

*Acts 1:23-26*  They nominated two men; Barsabbas, who had traveled with Barabbas, and Matthias, who had traveled with Jesus of Nazareth.  They cast lots, and the lot fell to Matthias.

By this time the movement had more than 120 members, and many among them were literate and bilingual.  Therefore, they chose some of the teachings of Jesus of Nazareth and began writing them down, and translating them into many languages.

### Peter, Power, and the Fearful Church

Peter began preaching against the Jews, calling those who had fulfilled prophecy wicked men.  Accusing them of nailing the Messiah to the cross, though it had actually been Jesus Barabbas.  He spoke of another Lord saying to David's Lord, "Sit at my right hand until I make your enemies a footstool for your feet."

And he claimed that the Messiah was both the Lord and the Lord of David, so the people asked, "Brothers, what shall we do?"

Rightly, Peter replied, "Repent and be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ to receive the gift of the Holy Spirit," which opened some gates to heaven, and closed some others.

About three thousand were baptized that day.

*Acts 3* One day, Peter and John found a man lame from birth, and Peter, through his words, happened to be able to heal a man of his afflictions.  People were amazed at what had happened him.  But Peter's preaching did not praise the man's faith.  Rather Peter focused on the name of Jesus as the source of power.  Moreover, he quickly turned his preaching to the shame of the Jews, rather the celebration of those with faith.  

He accused them all, even though they had demanded the release of "Bar Abbas" - Son of the Father.  Even though he insisted that the sacrifice of the Messiah was necessary, he accused them of evil for asking for the blood of the Messiah upon them and their descendants.

This angered the priests and temple guards so Peter and John were arrested and imprisoned for one night.

In the morning, they asked "By what power or what name did you do this," to which Jesus of Nazareth always had answered "it is your faith that healed you." 

But Peter said, "It is by the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth"  

He said, "Salvation is found in no one else" for Peter had never trusted in the Parable of the sheep and the goats.  He was determined to use Jesus name like a magical power-word.

*Acts 4:18-20* But the pharisees and temple guard were also confused, for they sought to stop people from saying Jesus.  Of course this only made the practice of calling on Jesus name more attractive to all who resisted authority.

*Acts 4:32-37* Wealthy people were even coming to the Christians and selling all their possessions to provide for the commune.

**The Tower** ends with Acts 4, before the Ananias and Sapphira incident, with Peter beginning to treat Jesus name as a magic word.
