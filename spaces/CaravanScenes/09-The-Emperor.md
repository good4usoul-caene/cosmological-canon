## Part 1: Bara Bas Returns from Egypt (26 CE)

Six months later, The seven arrived back from Egypt:  Jesusa Bara Bas—now calling herself Jesus Barabbas, traveled with Mary, Joseph, Didymus, and the three Magi:   Odys, Belzar, and Durvasini. They went to Sychar, to meet with the Upa Pandavas.   They traveled without the rest of the caravan, for now, traveling light.   Barabbas, now bearded, bore little resemblance to the feminized twelve-year-old Herod's men were searching for. 

As they came into Sychar, they exchanged their Egyptian clothes for traditional conservative Jewish garb.   They overheard Pharisees saying that with John in prison, Jesus' disciples were baptizing more than John ever had.  They asked about Jesus' disciples and were told there were twelve.  They needed to get to where John was imprisoned, but to stop in Sychar to gather the five Upa Pandavas.  Then they would have twelve for John.  

Near the field that Jacob gave to his son Joseph, Belzar said, "Don't forget the Library's gift for your mother."  He stopped, rummaging through the bags, and finding the scroll, highlighting the tales of Draupadi from the Mahabarta.  Jesus took the leather-bound scroll as they walked further toward town.  They approached the well, and Jesus said, "We're all hungry, but I'd like to rest a little bit."

"Savoring the moment?" asked Joseph.

"I'm a bit nervous.   I thought I would pray.   And perhaps glance over the scroll."

So six went into the village to buy some food, and he sat, first praying, and then opening up the scroll to read.  Soon a woman was approaching.   Her walk was nearly unmistakable.  It was his mother.   She walked up, nervously, taking him as a stranger, not even meeting his eyes.  She cranked the bucket down into the well, and brought up water to pour into her jar. 

Jesus said, "Please give me a drink."

The woman was surprised, for Jews refuse to have anything to do with Samaritans.  She said to Jesus, "You are a Jew, and I am a Samaritan woman. Why are you asking me for a drink?"

Jesus replied, tears already beginning to form, "If you only knew the gift God has for you and who you are speaking to, you would ask me, and I would give you living water."

"But sir, you don't have a rope or a bucket," she said, "and this well is very deep. Where would you get this living water?"

She turned to Jesus, seeing his eyes tearing up.   She squinted at him, with a moment of curiosity.  Why was he familiar?  Why did he seem important?  "And besides, do you think you're greater than our ancestor Jacob, who gave us this well?  How can you offer better water than he and his sons and his animals enjoyed?"

Jesus replied, "Anyone who drinks this water will soon become thirsty again.  But those who drink the water I give will never be thirsty again. It becomes a fresh, bubbling spring within them, giving them eternal life."

Now, Draupadi could see it.   Underneath the beard, was the face of someone she could barely have dreamed of seeing today.  Could it be Bara Bas?  The young girl who had loved her riddles?   A broad smile came across her face.  She pressed on, letting the riddle develop, seeing if Bara Bas with a beard hadn't lost her touch.

"Please, sir," Draupadi said, "give me this water!  Then I'll never be thirsty again, and I won't have to come here to get water."

Jesus saw the ruse was up.   "Go and get your husband," he told her. 

"I don't have one husband" the woman replied.

Jesus said, "You're right!  You don't have one husband— for you have five husbands, the father of your five boys, and you never married the sixth.  You certainly spoke the truth!"

21 Jesus replied, "Believe me, dear woman, the time is coming when it will no longer matter whether you worship the Father on this mountain or in Jerusalem. 22 You Samaritans know very little about the direction of creation, the one you worship, while we Jews know all about him, for "Jesus", the word for salvation comes from Hebrew, through the Jews. 23 But the time is coming—indeed it's here now—when true worshipers will worship the Father in spirit and in truth. The Father is looking for those who will worship him that way. 24 For God is Spirit, so those who worship him must worship in spirit and in truth."

The woman said, "I know the Messiah is coming—the one who is called Christ. When he comes, he will explain everything to us."

Then Jesus shrugged, "But I am is here.   I am the one speaking to you"

Without resolving a single riddle, Jesus and his mother cried together, sharing the living water of a happy reconnection, and the living water of blood relation.  Jesus gave her the scroll, and said, "This is a gift from Belzar.   It tells everything you accomplished in a past life."

Draupi smiled, wiping tears from her eyes. "You haven't lost your touch, my child.   Even Egypt couldn't dull your wit."

The other disciples came back with food from Sychar, now also joined by Barabbas' five older brothers.  Barabbas and Draupi read the scroll together.  "I hope this tells me where I came from." Barabbas said, "Because I'm still not sure where I'm going."

Joseph, hearing this, said, "You're going to free John.  That's where you're going."

As Barabbas and his mother traded tawdry riddles, back and forth, the Upa Pandava's were a bit shocked, but none of them asked, "What do you want with her?" or "Why are you talking to her?"   

Draupi left her water jar beside the well and ran back to the village.  Prat picked it up and the five followed her into town as she was telling everyone, 29 "Come and see a man who told me everything I ever did!  Could he possibly be the Messiah?" 30 So the people came streaming from the village to see him.

Meanwhile, the others were urging Jesus, "Rabbi, eat something."

Jesus took the food, and began to speak another riddle.   "I have a kind of food you know nothing about."

The others took the signal in stride, slipping into performance mode.  "Did someone bring him food while we were gone?" the disciples asked each other, playing their parts.

"My food," said Jesus, "is to do the will of him who sent me and to finish his work.  Don't you have a saying, 'It's still four months until harvest'? I tell you, open your eyes and look at the fields! They are ripe for harvest. Even now the one who reaps draws a wage and harvests a crop for eternal life, so that the sower and the reaper may be glad together. Thus the saying 'One sows and another reaps' is true. I sent you to reap what you have not worked for. Others have done the hard work, and you have reaped the benefits of their labor." (John 4:35-38)

The others worked at the riddle for a time and tried to make it more performance-worthy.

### Tabula Rasa

**John 4:40** So when the Samaritans came to him, they urged him to stay with them, and he stayed two days.

Didymus thought of the phrase, "Nickel, Nickel, Dime, ...." "Measured, found wanting." The writing on the wall. Like Daniel, he was preparing to project a message onto the wall for a superstitious audience.

Unlike Daniel, the subject of his miracle was not to be condemned, but celebrated as the Savior of the world. 

"Jesus" they would call him.  The Hebrew word for Salvation. 

Barabbas stepped up to the front of the shrine.

Didymus arranged the candle so that it would light up his parchment, and the lens so that it would project the images onto the wall. He wished the candle were brighter, and the lens were more refined, but it would have to do. 

Barabbas stepped up to the podium and touched the scroll in his pocket. It was blank except for the words written in Samaritan, "Jesus means Salvation". It was written so small that even the youngest might have trouble to see.

Barabbas stepped up to the dais and began to speak in metaphor. 

>"If I am a word, then how do you know what I mean?   
If I am a ladder, am I the way up, or the way down.  
If I am silence, am I nothing, or am I the space between words?  
If I am a stream, am I the earth, or am I the water?  
If I am fire, am I the stick, or am I the ash?  
If I am an answer, then what is the question?  
If I am a question, then what is the answer. 

>I am the fence, that gives your sheep protection at night.  
I am the pasture that gives them a place to feed in the day.   
I am the gate that opens and closes between them.  
I am the little baby that brings joy to the parents.  
I am the little baby that only takes and never gives back.  
I am the riddle to whom you have never found an answer.  
I am the answer that you have never questioned.

>I am a gentle winding path through the woods  
that you take because you are going nowhere.  
I am the great trade route from Alexandria to the far east  
that you take because you have places to be. 

>I am the envelope that you must rip open to find out what's inside.  
I am the paper, on which anything can be written.  
I am scripture, that which wise men have written  
in order to understand the heart and mind of God.   
And I am a dirty limerick that schoolboys exchange and snicker.

>I am the myth that the bards tell to teach and entertain.  
I am the truth that the seekers have not yet found.  
I am the light by which they see to walk.  
I am the letters in a book not yet written.

>I am the shame of prostitutes,  
and the pride of kings and warriors. 

>I am the way, that which any may choose to take.  
I am the truth, all that has ever happened, and all that ever will be.  
I am the light.  The ability to see clearly.

He paused, lifting a finger to his lips to silence them before they could answer.

Next, he demonstrated, taking a wire frame with two of Didymus' lenses, and lifting them up to hold securely around his nose.

He held the parchment up to read it.

On the paper was written: 

>I am scripture.  
I speak not with thunder or flame or stone.  
But the quiet voice that says, this is good.  This is right.  
Whether spoken to God or to no one.  
Whether written in ink, or in silence.  
Scripture is the earnest pursuit of what ought to be. 

He took the paper to his grandfather, Draupa, who hadn't read in two decades.  
He put the lenses over his eyes, and with great astonishment, he haltingly read. 

When he was done, Barabbas returned to the podium and continued.

>I am your best eye, that gives your eye the ability to read.  
I am your right hand.  I am your ear, your tongue, your mind.

>I am the aqueduct that brings fresh water.   
I am a sewer that flushes away your waste.  
I am the candle that lets you see at night.  
I am the plough that lets the farmer till the soil.   
I am the oar and the sail, that pushes a boat through water.  
I am the winnowing fan that separates wheat from chaff.

>I am the riddle.  I am the Question.   
I am the answer. I am the Solution.  
I am invention. The capacity for man to find a way to make his way more easily.  
If I am invention, If I am an idea not yet written,  
if I am everything that makes life easier for you, then what do you call me? 

>I am Salvation

Then he took the other paper... In script so small even the best eyes couldn't read.  
Each literate man gave the glasses a try, and found they could see the text:

>"Jesus means salvation"

**John 4:41** And because of Barabbas' words many more became believers.

### Bara Bas Returns from Egypt (26 CE)

In the week before Salome's Bat Mitzvah, they made themselves known as John's disciples.  Mary, Joseph, Didymus, Odys, Belzar, Durvasini with her. They met with Prat, Soma, Karman, Nika, and Sena in Nazareth.  Joseph and Mary were impressed with the decor in their humble home. 

Gabriel met with them and counted heads. "Good.  For now, you will call yourselves John's disciples.  Bara, Herod wants you to turn yourself in in exchange for John."

Bara Bas said, "Then I will trade my freedom for his."

"No.  You will not," Gabriel said. "He would imprison you both.  Herodias has come up with a plan.  It's dangerous, but we think it will work.  Next week, at Salome's Bat Mitzvah, she will ask for John's head. I will pretend to perform the execution.  He will appear dead, but I will bring his body to you. The poison will wear off, and he will awaken. You will shave his beard, and he will never again be known as John the Baptist."

### The Twelve Assembled (26 CE)

The twelve came to where John was imprisoned.  

On the night of Salome's Bat Mitzvah, they found a local cemetery and arranged a place to hide John while they buried and mourned an empty coffin. 

1. **Jesusa Bara Bas** (Jesus Barabbas)
2. **Mary** (Mother of Messiah)
3. **Joseph** (Foster father of Jesus of Nazareth)
4. **Didymus** (Thomas, the lens-seller)
5. **Odys** (Magi #1, the Vintner)
6. **Belzar** (Magi #2, the Astrologer)
7. **Durvasini** (Magi #3, the Healer)
8. **Prativindhya** (Upa Pandava #1, Prat)
9. **Sutasoma** (Upa Pandava #2, Soma)
10. **Shrutakarma** (Upa Pandava #3, Karman)
11. **Shatanika** (Upa Pandava #4, Nika)
12. **Shrutasena** (Upa Pandava #5, Sena)

That night, Gabriel wheeled John out of the prison on what looked like a food-cart. His head stuck out, covered in blood. Pig guts surrounded his neck, hiding the hole in the platter.  They wheeled John out of sight of any passersby, then pulled him out of the cart and put him in the coffin.

From there, all twelve took the coffin and carried it all the way to the cemetery.  John's body was dumped into the secret place, and they mourned and buried the coffin, as onlookers mourned with them.

Once the onlookers had gone away, they went to John's secret place and found him awakening. With a great set of shears, they chopped off his beard, and they gave him a basin of water and a straight-edge to shave off the rest. 

And John the Apostle came with them out of the cemetery.

## Part 3: The Plan Revealed (26 CE)

### Herodias and Salome's Conversation with John (Months Before the Execution)

One evening, Herodias brought Gabriel to John's cell.   Salome came as well, her face shadowed by the torchlight. 

"John," Herodias said, her voice low, "you know the only claim to prophecy you have over Salome is that you were born male."

John looked up from his stone bench, his eyes tired but clear. "Honestly, this is the first I'm hearing about the whole Maher Shalal Hash Baz thing." He glanced at Salome, then back at Herodias. "If Salome wants it, she can have it. But I'm just grateful you're getting me out of here."

Salome stepped forward, her hands clasped tightly. "So you're not upset that I'll be asking for your head on a platter?"

John smiled faintly. "Not if it means I get to leave this place."

Herodias placed a hand on Salome's shoulder. "This is dangerous, John. If the ruse fails, Salome will be remembered as a murderer. And you—" She paused.   "—you could actually die."

John stood, walking closer to the bars. "I've been here long enough to know that death is not the worst thing.   But being forgotten—being silent—that is worse."

Salome's voice was steady, but her eyes shone.   "I won't let you be forgotten.  I'll make sure they remember—not the Baptist who was killed, but the Apostle who was freed."

Gabriel, who had been silent, finally spoke. "Then it's settled. We'll stage the execution.  But John—when you walk out of that cemetery, you must never be John the Baptist again."

John nodded.   "I know.  I'll shave the beard.   I'll walk with Bara Bas.   And I'll wait for the one who is to come."

## Back in Sychar

On the second day, it was Purim. Belzar once again turned water into wine, and Jesus Barabbas became very drunk. The next morning, he awoke with a hangover, saying "Oh, my...   a prophet has no honor in his own country." Yet the Samaritans welcomed him.   The troupe had breakfast, and shook off their hangovers as well as they could, then headed towards Nazareth, where they were to meet with Gabriel.

---

### Gabriel's Visit to Jesus of Nazareth in Cana (John 4:43–50)

Meanwhile, Gabriel traveled 10 km to Cana where Jesus of Nazareth was staying. He said to him, "Jesus, I fear for the soul of my daughter. We have a plan to rescue John, but it will ruin her reputation forever.   Can you come down before my child dies?"

Jesus saw him.   He knew that he could do nothing by himself.  All he had ever done is see miracles, and acknowledge faith.  But he could see this man was in need.  "You are a man of authority."

"Not 'of'; I myself am a man under authority, with soldiers under me. I tell this one, 'Go,' and he goes; and that one, 'Come,' and he comes.   I say to my servant, 'Do this,' and he does it."

"So you need my authority, or you're putting me under your authority?"

"Sir, I know you perform miracles. But my daughter is not physically sick. She is morally imperiled. In one week, at Herod's birthday feast, she will ask for the head of John the Baptist on a platter.   Herodias has planned this to free John, but I fear for Salome's soul.   If the plan fails, she will be remembered forever as a murderer.   Please, assure me that she will live," said Gabriel.

"Truly I tell you, I have not found anyone in Israel with such great faith. I say to you that many will come from the east and the west, and will take their places at the feast with Abraham, Isaac and Jacob in the kingdom of heaven.  **Unless the people see signs and wonders, they will never believe.**"

Then Jesus said to the centurion, "Go!   Let it be done just as you believed it would.   Your daughter will live." And Gabriel's fear was healed at that moment.  The man took Jesus at his word and departed.  

As he left, Jesus' disciple heard him say, "But, for a time, the subjects of the kingdom will be thrown outside, into the darkness, where there will be weeping and gnashing of teeth."

This was the first sign Jesus performed after coming from Judea to Galilee.

### Gabriel's Conversation with John (Final Details of the Plan)

Gabriel stepped forward. "I'll make sure the ruse is convincing.   Pig guts, blood, the whole show."

John nodded. "When do we do this?"

"At Herod's birthday feast," Herodias said.  "Gabriel will be on duty that night. It's the only time we can guarantee the plan will work."

John looked at Salome. "And you're sure you can do this?"

Salome's face was steady. "I've been training for this my whole life. My mother has taught me rhetoric, philosophy, and the arts of persuasion. I can ask for your head, and Herod will be bound by his oath."

Gabriel said, "Once you're out, we'll shave your beard and you'll never be known as John the Baptist again. You'll be free."

John narrowed his eyes. "And you think they won't notice me breathing?"

Gabriel said, "No, but you might not like why.   I'm going to be giving you a sort of poison.  One that will paralyze you and slow your breath to a point nearly undetectable. It will be convincing for the half hour that I cart you around the room, but it might take you a week or two to fully recover."

Herodias said, "And if the dose is too high?"

Gabriel said, "If the dose is too low, they'll catch you breathing. If the dose is too high, you'll suffocate."

John said, "Maybe you could convince them in 10 or 20 minutes instead of a half-hour?   I kinda like breathing."

Herodias said, "Yes, John, you must survive."

## Part 4: The Execution of the Plan (26 CE)

### Salome's Dance (Herod's Birthday Feast)

The night of Herod's birthday feast arrived. The hall was filled with guests, music, and wine. 

Salome danced, her movements graceful and captivating. When she finished, Herod called out, "Ask me for anything, and I will give it to you!   Whatever you ask, I will give you, up to half my kingdom!"

Herodias leaned close to Salome and whispered, "Ask for John's head on a platter.   Your father will be horrified, but bound by his oath.   We'll stage the execution—John will be freed."

Salome stepped forward.  "I want the head of John the Baptist on a platter."

The hall fell silent. Herod's face went pale. "Salome...   anything but that."

"You swore an oath," Salome said calmly. 

Herod looked around at his guests, then nodded slowly. "Very well.   Bring me John's head."

### Gabriel's Ruse (The Staged Execution)

In the back room, Gabriel worked quickly. He had prepared a rolling cart with a hole cut into the top, large enough for a man's head to fit through. 

He brought John to the cart and said, "Lie down.   Your head will stick up through the hole.   I'll smear pig guts and blood around your neck.  It will look like your head has been severed."

John lay down on the cart, his head sticking up through the hole. Gabriel smeared pig guts and blood around John's neck, creating the illusion of a severed head on a platter.

He checked John's nose to make sure it was clear.   "Breathe slowly.  Don't move.  I'll wheel you into the hall, and then I'll bring you out to the disciples."

John nodded, his face covered in blood.  

---



### The Tense Moment (Night of the Execution)

That night, Gabriel wheeled John out of the prison on what looked like a food-cart.  His head stuck out, covered in blood.   Pig guts surrounded his neck, hiding the hole in the platter.

Gabriel wheeled the cart into the banquet hall.  The guests recoiled at the sight.    Salome took the "head" with steady hands and presented it to Herod. 

"It is done," she said. 

Herod stared at the grotesque display, his stomach churning. "Take it away."

But just as Gabriel began to wheel the cart toward the door, a loud, unmistakable sound erupted from beneath the cart—a long, wet fart that echoed through the hall. 

The room froze. One of the guards squinted at the "severed head."  
"Wait—did it just—"

Before anyone could finish, Herodias burst into the hall, her voice sharp and commanding.  

"GET THAT DISGUSTING THING OUT OF MY BALLROOM AT ONCE!" she shrieked, waving her hands dramatically.  "I will NOT have my guests subjected to this barbarism a moment longer!   OUT!   OUT!"

The guards scrambled, Gabriel wheeled the cart swiftly through the doorway, and the hall erupted in murmurs.   Herod, still pale, waved dismissively. 

"Yes, yes, take it away.   Herodias is right.  This is unseemly."

---

### The Handoff (Gabriel Brings John to the Disciples)

And just like that, John was gone—wheeled into the night, where the Twelve were waiting. 

Outside, Gabriel pulled John out of the cart and placed him into a coffin. "The poison will wear off soon.  Take him to the cemetery."

---

### The Burial (The Twelve Mourn)

From there, all twelve took the coffin and carried it all the way to the cemetery. John's body was dumped into a secret place, and they mourned and buried an empty coffin as onlookers mourned with them.

Once the onlookers had gone away, they went to John's secret place and found him awakening. With a great set of shears, they chopped off his beard, and they gave him a basin of water and a straight-edge to shave off the rest.  

And John the Apostle came with them out of the cemetery.  

---

### The Journey to Capernaum (The Twelve Take John to Safety)

The Twelve packed John onto a camel and traveled to Capernaum, a fishing town on the northern shore of the Sea of Galilee, about 40 km from Cana.  

Belzar, the astrologer, tended to John's recovery. "You're safe now," he said. "No one will recognize you without your beard."

John looked at his reflection in a basin of water. "I'm no longer John the Baptist," he said quietly. "I'm just... John."

Bara Bas smiled. "You're John the Apostle now.   Welcome."

---

## Part 5: The Second Sign (26 CE)

### Odys Travels to Ein Karem (Zechariah's Home)

A few days after John's staged execution, Odys, the vintner, traveled to Ein Karem to inform Zechariah of his son's condition. 

Zechariah was sitting in his garden when Odys arrived.  

"Zechariah," Odys said, "I bring news of your son."

Zechariah looked up, his face tense.   "Is he alive?"

"Yes," Odys said. "But it's touch-and-go. He's recovering from the poison Gabriel used to stage his execution.    We've taken him to Capernaum, but his condition is fragile."

Zechariah stood.   "I must go to him."

Odys said, "There's something else. Jesus of Nazareth is in Cana. Gabriel spoke with him, and Jesus assured him that his daughter would live.  Perhaps Jesus can assure you that your son will live as well."

Zechariah nodded.   "Then I will go to Cana."

---

### Zechariah's New Role (Roman Political Figure)

Zechariah had once been a priest, but after the birth of John, he had transitioned into a Roman political role. He now served as a **royal official** in Herod's court, overseeing census records and administrative matters. 

This transition had been difficult for Zechariah, who had always been a man of faith. But after witnessing the corruption of the temple priests, he had come to believe that serving Rome might be a way to protect his people from within.

Now, as he traveled to Cana, he wondered if his decision had been wise. 

---

### Zechariah Travels to Cana (Seeking Jesus of Nazareth)

Zechariah arrived in Cana and found Jesus of Nazareth teaching in the town square. 

"Rabbi," Zechariah said, approaching him, "I am Zechariah, father of John the Baptist. I have heard that you assured Gabriel that his daughter would live.    I come to you now to ask for the same assurance for my son.    He is recovering from poison in Capernaum, and I fear for his life."

Jesus looked at Zechariah steadily. "You people will never believe unless you see signs and wonders," he said. 

Zechariah said, "I don't need a sign, Rabbi. I only need your word.  Tell me that my son will live, and I will believe."

Jesus said, "Go.    Your son will live."

---

### The Second Sign (John 4:51–54)

While Zechariah was still on the way back to Capernaum, his servants met him with the news that his boy was living. 

When he inquired as to the time when his son got better, they said to him, "Yesterday, at one in the afternoon, the fever left him."

Then the father realized that this was the exact time at which Jesus had said to him, "Your son will live."

So he and his whole household believed.

This was the second sign Jesus performed after coming from Judea to Galilee.
