**Baby Riddle** 34 “My food,” said Jesus, “is to do the will of him who sent me and to finish his work. 35 Don’t you have a saying, ‘It’s still four months until harvest’? I tell you, open your eyes and look at the fields! They are ripe for harvest. 36 Even now the one who reaps draws a wage and harvests a crop for eternal life, so that the sower and the reaper may be glad together. 37 Thus the saying ‘One sows and another reaps’ is true. 38 I sent you to reap what you have not worked for. Others have done the hard work, and you have reaped the benefits of their labor.”

The father sends a baby to reap what it has not worked for.  The mother does the hard work and the baby reaps the benefits of their labor.

#### Tabula Rasa

John 4:40 So when the Samaritans came to him, they urged him to stay with them, and he stayed two days. 

Didymus thought of the phrase, "Nickel, Nickel, Dime, ...." "Measured, found wanting." The writing on the wall. Like Daniel, he was preparing to project a message onto the wall for a superstitious audience. 

Unlike Daniel, the subject of his miracle was not to be condemned, but celebrated as the Savior of the world. 

"Jesus" they would call him. The Hebrew word for Salvation. 

Barabbas stepped up to the front of the shrine. 

Didymus arranged the candle so that it would light up his parchment, and the lens so that it would project the images onto the wall. He wished the candle were brighter, and the lens were more refined, but it would have to do. 

Barabbas stepped up to the podium and touched the scroll in his pocket. It was blank except for the words written in Samaritan, "Jesus means Salvation".  It was written so small that even the youngest might have trouble to see. 

Barabbas stepped up to the dais and began to speak in metaphor.

>"If I am a word, then how do you know what I mean? 
If I am a ladder, am I the way up, or the way down. 
If I am silence, am I nothing, or am I the space between words? 
If I am a stream, am I the earth, or am I the water? 
If I am fire, am I the stick, or am I the ash? 
If I am an answer, then what is the question? 
If I am a question, then what is the answer. 

>I am the fence, that gives your sheep protection at night. 
I am the pasture that gives them a place to feed in the day. 
I am the gate that opens and closes between them. 
I am the little baby that brings joy to the parents. 
I am the little baby that only takes and never gives back. 
I am the riddle to whom you have never found an answer. 
I am the answer that you have never questioned. 

>I am a gentle winding path through the woods 
that you take because you are going nowhere. 
I am the great trade route from Alexandria to the far east 
that you take because you have places to be. 

> I am the envelope that you must rip open to find out what's inside. 
I am the paper, on which anything can be written. 
I am scripture, that which wise men have written 
in order to understand the heart and mind of God. 
And I am a dirty limerick that schoolboys exchange and snicker. 

>I am the myth that the bards tell to teach and entertain. 
I am the truth that the seekers have not yet found. 
I am the light by which they see to walk. 
I am the letters in a book not yet written. 

>I am the shame of prostitutes, 
and the pride of kings and warriors. 

> I am the way, that which any may choose to take. 
I am the truth, all that has ever happened, and all that ever will be. 
I am the light. The ability to see clearly. 

He paused, lifting a finger to his lips to silence them before they could answer.

Next, he demonstrated, taking a wire frame with two of Didymus' lenses, and lifting them up to hold securely around his nose.

He held the parchment up to read it. 

On the paper was written, "

>I am scripture.   
I speak not with thunder or flame or stone.   
But the quiet voice that says, this is good. This is right. 
Whether spoken to God or to no one.   
Whether written in ink, or in silence.  
Scripture is the earnest pursuit of what ought to be."   

He took the paper to his grandfather, Draupa, who hadn't read in two decades 
He put the lenses over his eyes, and with great astonishment, he haltingly read.  

When he was done, Barabbas  returned to the podium and continued.


>I am your best eye, that gives your eye the ability to read. 
I am your right hand.  I am your ear, your tongue, your mind.  

>I am the aqueduct that brings fresh water. 
I am a sewer that flushes away your waste. 
I am the candle that lets you see at night. 
I am the plough that lets the farmer till the soil 
I am the oar and the sail, that pushes a boat through water. 
I am the winnowing fan that separates wheat from chaff. 

>I am the riddle.  I am the Question
I am the answer.  I am the Solution
I am invention. The capacity for man to find a way to make his way more easily. 
If I am invention, If I am an idea not yet written, 
if I am everything that makes life easier for you, then what do you call me?

>I am Salvation

Then he took the other paper...  In script so small even the best eyes couldn't read.  
Each literate man gave the glasses a try, and found they could see the text:

>"Jesus means salvation"

John 4:41 And because of Barabbas' words many more became believers.
