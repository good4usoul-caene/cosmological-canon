Begin **John  21**

**1** Jesus of Nazareth lived in Damascus at the time when Peter was released, and he got word that Peter was free and that Herod had allegedly been killed by an angel of the Lord.  He decided to make one more visit to his disciples near the Sea of Galilee which had become the Sea of Tiberias under Herod's rule.  

**2** They were together.  Simon Peter to whom Jesus had said, "I give you the keys to the Kingdom," and "Get behind me, Satan,"; Nathanael from Cana, the guard who had claimed to pierce Jesus' side, the sons of Zebedee, who were actually the Upa Pandavas, and two others of his disciples.  
**3** Late in the evening, Simon Peter said to them, "I am going to fish".  They said "we also will come with you."  They went forth and went up into the boat and caught nothing in the night.

**4** With the morning, Jesus of Nazareth stood on the shore in the distance.  However the disciples did not know it was Jesus.  

**5** Then, Jesus said to them, "children, have you not caught any fish?"  He was speaking literally and metaphorically, as he had earlier told them, 'I will make you fishers of men'.

Interpretations
(1) Jesus saw Peter's "yes" clarification. Peter's love was not agape, but phileo.  His love was at least somewhat self-serving.

**The Moon** - John, Salome, and possibly Mary reflect upon Peter's behavior.  How he has taken up the equipment of the foolish shepherd.  They do not feel that Peter's love for Jesus is unconditional, because Jesus would have never shamed someone to death for simply failing to give over all of their money to the commune.  Now that that sort of evil is present in the commune, they are catching a lot of fish, but Peter's Jesus takes those fish and chars them black before feeding them to his disciples.  John accuses Peter of only having phileo love for Jesus of Nazareth, but hopes that someday, someone will dress Peter as a better man than he actually is.  (Someday someone will put on your clothes and take you in a direction you do not want to go)
