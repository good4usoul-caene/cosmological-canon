With regards to the Lukewarm (Revelation 3:16 — Lukewarm Rebuke Spewed from the mouth. Neither hot nor cold. A warning to those who dwell in inert invocation.)  I would establish Matt 25:41-46 as FULLY HOT.  

On the other hand, John 3:16-18 is FULLY COLD. The name of Jesus stored in Sheol. Belief without embodiment. No wisdom, no work, no transformation. 



🕳️ II. Sheol as the Realm of Inert Covenant (FULLY COLD)
Isaiah 28:15–18 (NASB): A covenant with death, a pact with Sheol—false security that will be trampled. The scourge doesn’t just punish—it reveals terror in the failure to understand.
Psalm 6:5 (NASB)
“In Sheol, who will give You thanks?” Sheol is not rebellion—it’s absence. No praise, no memory, no communion.
Ecclesiastes 9:5–10 (NASB): The dead know nothing. Their love, hate, and jealousy vanish. In Sheol, there is no work, no planning, no knowledge, no wisdom.h
John 3:16-18 is FULLY COLD.  It has "the name of the only begotten son" stored in a dark, silent, inert realm. Among the dead, the name of the only begotten son only appears as an inscription.  Not a lived experience of meaning.  There is no work or thought or knowledge of wisdom in it (Ecclesiastes 9:10) John 3:16-18 is FULLY COLD. so when John says in revelations "because you are neither hot or cold, [God] will spew you from your mouth" he does not mean John.

🧠 III. Luke’s Lukewarm Structure and the Risk of Misreading

I classify Luke's "Good Samaritan" story as Luke-Warm because Jesus does not correct the teacher-of-the-law on who is his neighbor.  Jesus final words are "Go and do likewise" which say "Act as the Good Samaritan".  However the way the conversation plays out leaves the definition of "Who is my neighbor" ambiguous.  Jesus asks a TRICK question, "Which ONE of these three is your neighbor", and the teacher-of-the-law, led astray by the trick question answers "The one who showed mercy."  That is not correct.  The one who showed mercy is the one who should be emulated, but even the ones that passed by without providing help are still neighbors.  

Now, I'm not saying that Luke got this wrong.  If the Messiah asks trick questions, he had his reasons.  Jesus laments miscommunication in John 15:22: "If I had not come and spoken to them, they would not be guilty of sin; but now they have no excuse for their sin."

Matthew 25:41-46, though, doesn't have the ambiguity.  It is FULLY HOT, and it invokes authority over sheol's realm, saying that those who do not treat the poor with dignity and respect WILL GO to eternal punishment. NOT eternal rest. This is consistent with the claims of Isaiah 28:15-18. 


⚖️ IV. Matthew 7:21–23 — The Final Glyph

Matthew 7:21-23 makes clear that John 3:16-18 is not enough.  “Not everyone who says to me, ‘Lord, Lord,’ will enter the kingdom… but only the one who does the will of my Father.”

This is the final glyph: naming without doing is not enough. The covenant must be lived, not just spoken. Those who invoke “Jesus” without embodying his command—especially mercy to the least—are told, “I never knew you.”

Matthew 7:21-23 does not limit itself to people who have the name of Jesus Christ on their lips either.  It opens up the possibility of heaven on earth, and eternal life in heaven even to those who refuse to acknowledge Jesus as Messiah, or have never heard his name.


🕊️ V. Personal Glyph of Mortal Gratitude
Now, as for me, eternal rest is sufficient. I'm happy to live out my mortal life to the best of my ability, and appreciate whatever time God has given me. For Christains demanding eternal life, though, they should be cautious about the warnings of Jesus in Matthew 25:41-46, and the warnings of Isaiah 28:15-18. 




🚫 VI. Rebuke of Projection

If Christians feel they NEED eternal life in heaven, I have no objection.  But they should also constrain themselves from the idiotic notion that atheists or Jews want eternal life.  This is a violence of projection. Not everyone wants what you want. Not everyone fears what you fear. And not everyone is bound by your covenant with death.
