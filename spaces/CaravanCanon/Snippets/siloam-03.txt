''John 9:''  Jesus Barabbas heals a blind man.

Since the feeding of 5000, [[Jesus of Nazareth]] went to Sidon, Tyre, and back to Galilee.  However [[Jesus Barabbas]] stayed in Jerusalem and the surrounding area.  

//1 As he went along, he saw a man blind from birth. 2 His disciples asked him, “Rabbi, who sinned, this man or his parents, that he was born blind?”//

Here, the disciples and Jesus are having a discussion of the Final Cause (see the [[Four Causes]]) of a man's blindness.  Their premise is that ordinarily, the blindness of a child punishes the sins of the parents.  Jesus Barabbas does not correct their premise, but states that this is a special case:

//3 “Neither this man nor his parents sinned,” said Jesus, “but this happened so that the works of God might be displayed in him. //

1. A plain reading here suggests that "The works of God" are the things that Jesus is about to do to heal the man.  2. However, taken holistically, the works of God are every event that happens anywhere in the universe.  3. It could also be that John is playing a literary trick.  If this chapter of John is fictional, then John has introduced the blind character, and caused him to be born, for the purpose of fulfilling his role in the text.  4. Finally, it may be that Jesus intends to fulfill the instructions given by the "Sovereign Lord" of Ezekiel, which he here calls "the works of God."

//4 As long as it is day, we must do the works of him who sent me. Night is coming, when no one can work. 5 While I am in the world, I am the light of the world.”//

Ezekiel 12:3, Sovereign Lord tells Ezekiel, "During the daytime, while they are watching, pack your bags for exile."

//6 After saying this, he spit on the ground, made some mud with the saliva, and put it on the man’s eyes.//

In Ezekiel 12:5, Sovereign Lord says to Son of Man, "Dig through the wall, and bring forth your belongings through it."  At this time, Jesus Barabbas was traveling without belongings, so he dug through the ground and spat in it.  His only belonging was his saliva, so he brought his saliva through the wall.

//7 “Go,” he told him, “wash in the Pool of Siloam” (this word means “Sent”). So the man went and washed, and came home seeing.//

There are two possibilities here.  Either, (1) the man went to the Pool of Siloam which was near the temple in Jerusalem, which is not mentioned in the other gospels, or (2) the man went to Bethsaida and found Jesus of Nazareth, which is described in Mark 8:22-25.  Whatever the case, John reports that the man was not healed directly by Jesus Barabbas, but went to another location to be healed.  

//8 His neighbors and those who had formerly seen him begging asked, “Isn’t this the same man who used to sit and beg?” 9 Some claimed that he was.

Others said, “No, he only looks like him.”

But he himself insisted, “I am the man.”

10 “How then were your eyes opened?” they asked.

11 He replied, “The man they call Jesus made some mud and put it on my eyes. He told me to go to Siloam and wash. So I went and washed, and then I could see.”

12 “Where is this man?” they asked him.

“I don’t know,” he said.//

When the man's neighbors ask, he is clear, and tells them that his eyes were healed when he washed in the Siloam.  

//13 They brought to the Pharisees the man who had been blind. 14 Now the day on which Jesus had made the mud and opened the man’s eyes was a Sabbath. 15 Therefore the Pharisees also asked him how he had received his sight. “He put mud on my eyes,” the man replied, “and I washed, and now I see.”//

When the Pharisees ask, the man does not specifically mention Siloam.  

//16 Some of the Pharisees said, “This man is not from God, for he does not keep the Sabbath.”

But others asked, “How can a sinner perform such signs?” So they were divided.

17 Then they turned again to the blind man, “What have you to say about him? It was your eyes he opened.”

The man replied, “He is a prophet.”//

A prophet predicts things about the future.  He does not make them happen.

//18 They still did not believe that he had been blind and had received his sight until they sent for the man’s parents. 19 “Is this your son?” they asked. “Is this the one you say was born blind? How is it that now he can see?”

20 “We know he is our son,” the parents answered, “and we know he was born blind. 21 But how he can see now, or who opened his eyes, we don’t know. Ask him. He is of age; he will speak for himself.” 22 His parents said this because they were afraid of the Jewish leaders, who already had decided that anyone who acknowledged that Jesus was the Messiah would be put out of the synagogue. 23 That was why his parents said, “He is of age; ask him.”//

The parents knew that the man's eyes had not been healed directly by Jesus Barabbas.  They knew he had gone to "the sent".  But they, like the blind man, did not want to mention "the sent" because they knew anyone who acknowledged that Jesus was the Messiah would be put out of the synagogue.

//24 A second time they summoned the man who had been blind. “Give glory to God by telling the truth,” they said. “We know this man is a sinner.”

25 He replied, “Whether he is a sinner or not, I don’t know. One thing I do know. I was blind but now I see!”

26 Then they asked him, “What did he do to you? How did he open your eyes?”

27 He answered, “I have told you already and you did not listen. Why do you want to hear it again? Do you want to become his disciples too?”//

The blind man has already told them that the man did not open his eyes.  He has told them that when he washed his eyes, they were opened.  But because the Pharisees have not listened, he asks them "Do you want to become disciples to the one who rubs mud in my eyes?"

//28 Then they hurled insults at him and said, “You are this fellow’s disciple! We are disciples of Moses! 29 We know that God spoke to Moses, but as for this fellow, we don’t even know where he comes from.”//

The Pharisees know that Jesus of Nazareth is from Nazareth.  But they have no idea where Jesus Barabbas is from.

//30 The man answered, “Now that is remarkable! You don’t know where he comes from, yet he opened my eyes.//

The blind man has given up on making the distinction.  He now says that Jesus Barabbas opened his eyes.  

//31 We know that God does not listen to sinners. He listens to the godly person who does his will. //

The blind man now testifies against God, that God does not listen to sinners.

//32 Nobody has ever heard of opening the eyes of a man born blind. 33 If this man were not from God, he could do nothing.”//

The blind man has decided to be a disciple for Jesus Barabbas.

//34 To this they replied, “You were steeped in sin at birth; how dare you lecture us!” And they threw him out.//

The blind man is now exiled.  The Pharisees are still convinced that the sins of the parents are put on the child.  

//35 Jesus heard that they had thrown him out, and when he found him, he said, “Do you believe in the Son of Man?”//

The book of Ezekiel refers often to "ben adam" which means "human being".  However, for a long time, this has been translated to "Son of Man."

//36 “Who is he, sir?” the man asked. “Tell me so that I may believe in him.”//

The blind man asks for information about the Son of Man.  

//37 Jesus said, “You have now seen him; in fact, he is the one speaking with you.”//

"Son of Man" taken as contronym could mean "Son of God, who is not a man" or "Son of a woman, but not a man."  It could also be taken literally, as a male child of a male father, in which case, the blind man is also "Son of Man".  Or, it could be anyone who is trying to obey the "Sovereign Lord" of Ezekiel, who speaks often to the "Son of Man."

If the reader follows the "Spirit of Truth" then we must strike the interpretation of the contronym.  That leaves us with two other possibilities.  

One possibility is that the blind man is talking to himself.  He could, for instance, be looking into a pool of water for the first time, seeing his own reflection.  He is saying "I see the Son of Man."  He sees the motion of his own lips in the reflection and is struck with awe and says.  "I am the one speaking with you."

If Jesus Barabbas is here, speaking for one who follows the instructions of the "Sovereign Lord" of Ezekiel, then Jesus has brought the blind man into exile with him, as a belonging.

//38 Then the man said, “Lord, I believe,” and he worshiped him.

39 Jesus said,[a] “For judgment I have come into this world, so that the blind will see and those who see will become blind.”//

Ezekiel 12:6 says "Cover your face so that you cannot see the land, for I have made you a sign to the Israelites."  So we can imagine that Jesus Barabbas covers his face with sack cloth and says "the blind will see and those who see will become blind."

//40 Some Pharisees who were with him heard him say this and asked, “What? Are we blind too?”//

Ezekiel 12:9  Did the Israelites ask Jesus Barabbas "What are you doing?"  No, they did not.  They saw him cover his head with sack-cloth, and say, "those who see will become blind."

Ezekiel 12:11, Barabbas says "I am a sign to you...  As I have done, so it will be done to you.  You will go into exile as captives."

//41 Jesus said, “If you were blind, you would not be guilty of sin; but now that you claim you can see, your guilt remains.//

The other interpretation is more playful.  The blind man is talking to himself.  He mourns his sight, because it has led to his exile, saying "For judgment I have come into this world, so that the blind will see and those who see will become blind."

The Pharisees do not see who he is talking to, so they ask "What, are we blind, too?"   

And the blind man, who is in exile for what Barabbas has done to him, spits out "If you were blind, you would not be guilty of sin."  For if they had not interrogated him, and seen him and judged him, they would not have been guilty of sin.  "But now that you claim you can see, your guilt remains." Because the pharisees claimed they could see the blind man who was healed, but they couldn't see that he had been exiled.
