What's not clear here:  Is the Sovereign Lord okay with prostitution, so long as the prostitutes are his?  He makes no judgement against them for prostitution, explicitly.  But rather, he objected when she defiled herself with the idols of those she lusted after.

In verses 20-21, specifically, there are body parts described; genitals, emission, bosom, and breasts, but no idols.  In this time, the Sovereign Lord does not say he was disgusted.

In Chapter 24, on 10/10/0009, Ezekiel's wife dies.  He also gives a parable about lewdness.  In the parable, the choice pieces of meat are put in the pot.  The best of the bones, the pick of the flock.   The meat is taken out, but the pot is encrusted, and "the deposit" will not go away.  

The next verse refers to "she"... Here, surely the most relevant "she" to the writer is his wife who has just died.  But whoever "she" is poured out her blood on the bare rock.  This could mean suicide.  She poured her blood on bare rock "to stir up wrath and take revenge."

Now, the meat from the pot is taken and cooked, and spiced.  But the bones in the pot are charred, and the pot, itself becomes so hot that its copper glows, and the impurities SHOULD be burned away, but the impurity of lewdness cannot be cleansed from the bones, and will never be cleansed from the Son of Man until the wrath of the Sovereign Lord has subsided (for example, if the Son of Man should die.)
