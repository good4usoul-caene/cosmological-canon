It’s an intriguing idea to imagine galactic filaments forming via primordial lightning bolts! While this isn’t supported by current scientific theories, let’s explore the concept creatively.

In this hypothetical scenario, we could imagine that in the early universe, immense electrical discharges, akin to lightning, sparked between regions of high-density matter. These discharges might have helped to shape the initial structures of the cosmic web, creating pathways of ionized gas and plasma that later evolved into the filaments we observe today.

As the universe expanded, these electrically charged regions could have stretched and cooled, causing the electrical activity to diminish. The increasing distances between galaxies would make it impossible for such electrical currents to continue flowing, as the vast separations would disrupt any potential conductive pathways.

While this idea is more science fiction than science fact, it does highlight the dynamic and complex processes that could have influenced the early universe. In reality, the formation of cosmic filaments is driven by gravitational forces and the distribution of dark matter, rather than electrical phenomena.
